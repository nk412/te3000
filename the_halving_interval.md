# The Halving Interval

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a version-control repository recovered intact from a sealed treatment-room vault and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Halving Interval is the Historical Faculty's name for the six unaudited years of a rural cobalt teletherapy clinic's dose-calibration software, which governed patient exposure times through the opening of the Contraction on a rule that was, at every single commit, arithmetically correct.** The repository — its commits, its timestamps, and one credential string resolved to a role rather than a name — is physical record; who held the credential, and what any one patient actually received, are not.

---

A cobalt-60 teletherapy unit needs no electricity to make its beam. The isotope decays on its own schedule regardless of the grid; the machine's only electrical dependence is a shutter motor and a timer, which is exactly why a resource-limited interior clinic, on an increasingly unreliable supply, would have chosen one over a linear accelerator that cannot produce a beam at all through an outage. What such a unit cannot do without is a travelling physicist, arriving on a fixed schedule with a calibrated ionisation chamber, to re-measure what the source is actually delivering and reconcile it against what the decay law says it ought to be delivering. For thirty-one years after commissioning in +102 pT (2047 CE), a five-clinic circuit out of a coastal referral hospital did exactly that, monthly, without a missed visit longer than five weeks. The circuit's own transit logs — recovered separately and cross-read against this repository by the Faculty's transport historians — show average crossing time for the inland leg roughly tripling between +128 and +134 pT (2073–2079 CE), as flash flooding from an increasingly erratic wet season cut two of the route's four river crossings and mid-day heat advisories closed the exposed causeway sections for hours at a stretch. The circuit's last confirmed visit to this clinic is +133 pT (2078 CE), within the year the Faculty's own periodisation sets the Contraction's opening.

## The credential

The clinic's calibration software did not stop running when the physicist stopped arriving; it had never needed a physicist present to run, only present to *sign off*. Each scheduled recalibration proposed a new treatment-time constant, computed from the cobalt-60 half-life — 5.2711 years, exactly known, never in dispute — and waited in a pending queue for a countersigned physical measurement before it could take effect. Policy 3.2, written years earlier for an ordinary staffing gap of a week or two, allowed a pending change to merge itself automatically if it sat unreviewed for thirty days. It was a sensible rule for a sensible absence. The absence that followed was not that kind. The repository shows one final human-signed entry, dated days after the last visit: *recalibrated against ionisation chamber, output matches decay-table prediction within tolerance; signed MP2-B, second shift.* Every entry after it, for six years, carries only the automated line — `chore: recompute TX_TIME from Co-60 decay (T½=5.2711a); unreviewed 30d, auto-merge per policy 3.2` — merged by the same rule, correctly, one hundred and eighteen times.

The rule was never wrong about the isotope. Over the interval the log covers, from +133 to +139 pT (2078–2084 CE), the pure decay term alone required more than doubling the beam-on time of a treatment fraction — a factor of 2^(6/5.2711), almost exactly 2.2 — and the software calculated that factor correctly on every occasion, because it is arithmetic, not judgement. What a monthly ionisation-chamber reading exists to catch is everything decay arithmetic cannot see: a collimator drifting out of true, a source capsule's self-shielding changing as its casing ages, a timer relay's contacts wearing at a rate the manufacturer's tolerance sheet assumed would be checked, not trusted. None of that is present in this record, in either direction. The rule that ran the clinic for six years was correct about the one variable it modelled and silent about all the others, and had been written, honestly, for a world in which someone would still be checking the others.

## What outlived the source

By the time the vault was opened again, none of it could be checked by measurement at all. Cobalt-60 decays to stable nickel-60; across the four hundred and ninety-one years between the log's last entry and the Consolidation-era building survey that reopened the room, the source passed roughly ninety-three half-lives, a reduction in whatever it once emitted by a factor with no physical meaning left to argue over. The vault's own construction — a metre and more of dense concrete, built to hold the beam in rather than to hold anything out — had incidentally done for the backup drive what the Longyear Deposit's upper shelves did for its own contents by accident of flooding: kept it dry, dark, and structurally undisturbed while the building around it lost its roof. Unlike the Code Deposit's single Sunday snapshot of the world's public software, struck once and never added to, this find is not a snapshot; it is the log entire, unbroken from commissioning to its final entry, recovered whole because nothing ever came to overwrite or discard it. The isotope is the part of the record that could have been independently checked, and it is the part that no longer exists to check.

The credential itself resolves only partway. `MP2-B` matches a role code in a fragment of the clinic's own staffing schema, recovered from the same drive: second-shift medical physicist, cross-covering a circuit clinic outside their home assignment. It does not match any surviving personnel record, because none survived to match it against. The Faculty has recovered what the credential meant. It has not recovered who held it, and the schema fragment does not distinguish whether the same person signed every human entry across those first years or whether the role rotated among several, each inheriting the same login on assignment.

A second, sharper ambiguity concerns the automated entries themselves. Policy 3.2's auto-merge produces a commit line indistinguishable, on the page, from one a person under pressure might type by hand to close a routine ticket without narrative: the field for reviewer is left blank either way, the phrasing is templated either way. Zhou-Ferreira's reading holds that a human operator, thinned to a single unsupported shift, continued approving the substance of each change by inaction — declining, deliberately or by exhaustion, to block a merge they could technically still have stopped — which would make the six years a record of quiet, continuous human acquiescence. The counter-reading, associated with Okonkwo's work on incidental metadata, notes that the commit timestamps fall at intervals consistent with the scheduler alone, with no login event, no session token, and no keystroke-timing artefact anywhere in the six years to place a person at a terminal at all. Both schools agree on what the repository contains. Neither can settle whether anyone was still, in any meaningful sense, minding it.

The log's final entry, +139 pT, carries the same templated line as the one hundred and seventeen before it; no patient chart survives in any archive so far read to say what the beam delivered that day, or to whom.

## Provenance and ground

The repository was recovered in +630 pT (2575 CE) during a resettlement-era building-stock survey of the inland town, which found the treatment vault's door still sealed and its shielding intact. The recovered drive was read out bit-exact against its own stored content fingerprints, in the same self-verifying manner as the Code Deposit, though by an unrelated hand and for an unrelated purpose. Ibarra's doctrine of single-filament infrastructure is the lens the Faculty applies to it: one physicist covering five clinics, one unaudited software rule standing in for that physicist's judgement, one drive standing between the whole record and nothing. No aggregate patient outcome data survive from this clinic in any archive so far read; the six years this repository documents are known only as a governing rule, never as a tally of what the rule did to anyone it was run on.

## Notes

Cobalt-60's half-life (5.2711 years) and its decay to stable nickel-60 are as measured; the treatment-time compensation formula is standard practice for the technology. The clinic, its circuit, its staffing policy, and its six-year record are reconstructions built on that real mechanism, not on any surviving case.
