The Doab Card Archive

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a shelf of chemically treated specimen cards and a paper field ledger recovered from anoxic river silt, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

The Doab Card Archive is the Historical Faculty’s name for the surviving specimen-card holdings of a regional livestock germplasm depot in a Gangetic doab, whose field workers sampled a landrace cattle population onto room-temperature DNA cards as the herds’ keepers left ahead of the heat, and which was buried under a river’s avulsion and read three thousand years later. The cards, the ledger, and the DNA they still carry are physical record; what the sampling was — routine stewardship, emergency salvage, or an artefact of one road out — is reconstruction, and the record does not settle it.

In a decade the enclosing sediment brackets to the +120s–140s pT (2060s–2080s CE), a livestock depot in the land between two rivers logged the last of its specimen cards and then stopped logging. The Doab Card Archive was a breed-conservation holding — punches of ear tissue and dried blood spots fixed onto treated cards to preserve the DNA of a regional landrace cattle population against its replacement by a few narrow high-yield commercial lines — filled in haste as the doab’s smallholders evacuated poleward and inland ahead of the wet-bulb crisis, and abandoned when the depot’s keepers left with them. The building’s cold stores failed within a few years of the grid, and the semen and embryos held in liquid nitrogen rotted, which is unremarkable. One annex of cards did not, and it is nearly all that remains. From it the Faculty recovers a landrace’s genome, a partial map of a displacement, and an argument in three parts about what the shelf represents that the missing part of the ledger would have decided. Of the cards the ledger records as filled, two in three still yield typeable DNA.

The depot and the erosion it was built against

The stake the depot was built to answer is a real and recurring one of the age: the collapse of a domestic species’ diversity onto a handful of production lines. Across the Combustion Age the region’s cattle and buffalo were a mosaic of local landraces — populations shaped over centuries to a particular heat, fodder, disease load, and draught use — and across the same age those landraces were being displaced by a small number of high-yield breeds bred for milk under managed conditions. Ibarra’s diagnosis of the period’s single-filament infrastructure names this among her standing examples, alongside one canal, one ore body, and the two poultry bloodlines onto which the age had narrowed the greater part of its egg and meat production: a planet running its food through dangerously few points of failure. A germplasm depot was the counter-measure — a place to bank the material of a vanishing landrace so that a population lost in the field could in principle be reconstituted from store.

The conservation arithmetic the depot worked under is likewise the period’s own. A minimum effective population size near fifty was held as the threshold below which a population suffers immediate inbreeding depression; a size nearer five hundred — some revisions argued a thousand — as the floor for long-term viability. The reason is a single identity: under drift, a population loses heterozygosity at about one part in twice its effective size per generation, so a population of effective size fifty sheds roughly one percent of its remaining variation each generation and one of size five hundred a tenth of that. To bank a landrace usefully was to bank enough distinct animals that the store would clear those thresholds. Whether the doab’s landrace, as it survives on the cards, does clear them is the first thing the archive is asked and cannot plainly answer.

The chemistry that did not need power

The cards are a standard sampling technology of the age, described here by function because the maker’s mark is not among what survives. Each is a fibrous matrix pre-treated with a surfactant that ruptures the cell, a chelating agent that binds the metal ions nucleases need to cut DNA, and a free-radical scavenger carried in a dye that marks a spot as filled; a drop of blood or a tissue punch pressed onto it lyses, fixes, and dries, and the DNA sits stable at room temperature with no cold chain at all. The age validated the method to its own satisfaction over years — controlled testing showed amplifiable DNA recoverable from such cards after eight to sixteen years at room temperature — and used it accordingly, as a way to move genetic material off the cold chain and onto a shelf.

The annex holding these cards survived because a delayed consignment of card stock had been shelved in a side room rather than the main vault, apart from the cryobank, and because the card chemistry needs no power to work. When the river avulsed and buried the depot, that side room went under anoxic silt — cold, dark, and without oxygen. Ancient-DNA taphonomy independently credits exactly those conditions with preserving amplifiable DNA across millennia in permafrost and anoxic sediment. The card matrix’s own chemistry, tested by its makers only across a decade or two, compounded by accident with a burial chemistry validated separately over far longer, and the cards outlasted their rated envelope by more than two orders of magnitude. This is an extrapolation from two real and separately established mechanisms meeting by chance in one room, not a property anyone designed into the cards.

What the cards and the ledger carry

From the recovered panel the Faculty reads the landrace directly. Runs of homozygosity — long unbroken homozygous stretches of a genome — give an inbreeding coefficient F as the fraction of the genome lying in such runs; mitochondrial haplotype diversity, a separate maternal-lineage tracer, counts the distinct founding female lines the sample holds. Read together they estimate how much of the landrace’s variation the shelf actually preserves. The paper ledger indexes the cards by collection zone, and its counts, set against what is typeable today, carry a section on their own.

The ledger’s marginal annotations are in the hands of local collectors, in the vernacular of the displaced, and they are an early written attestation of grammar the living tongue keeps: completed sampling is marked with a preverbal particle descended from the auxiliary done, written before the verb to note which animals of a herd had done been carded before the herd moved on, and one margin carries the invariant question tag whole. Philologists cite the ledger for it — a page of a working record catching, unawares, the completive aspect and the tag that are now diagnostic of Standard Terran’s descent from the age’s most-displaced vernaculars.

The reading

Three schools take the same secure facts to three unreconciled conclusions. Ibarra reads the archive as a single-filament case: a region’s entire landrace genetic future staked on one under-funded annex, its survival owed not to the system that built it but to a filing accident and a river, exactly the fragility her poultry-bloodline example describes. Zhou-Ferreira reads the same cards against the collapse thesis: the sampling is orderly, indexed, and internally consistent, the work of a program functioning as designed and continuing to function until the record simply stops — evidence of intention and continuity, not of panic. Ranganathan’s caution cuts across both. The typeable cards come overwhelmingly from herds along the one motorable route out of the doab, so animals sampled there are disproportionately relatives of one another; their shared runs of homozygosity depress the panel’s apparent diversity and inflate its F, and a landrace that may have cleared an effective size of fifty across the whole doab can be made to read as though it fell short of it, purely by where the road ran. On this reading the shelf cannot be taken as representative of the region at all. The record cannot adjudicate among the three, and the reason it cannot is specific: the ledger’s dispatch log — the pages that would show whether a given day’s collection was a scheduled round or an emergency run against a herd already leaving — is the part the burial and the years took, leaving the counts without their occasion.

Provenance and ground

The entry rests on the specimen-card annex and the field ledger of a single doab depot, recovered from anoxic channel silt where a river’s avulsion buried the building, and dated not by radiocarbon — unavailable for the age, per Ashworth-Nakamura, K. — but by luminescence on the sterile avulsion sand that sealed the room, which brackets the burial to the +120s–140s pT. Halvorsen, E. supplies the taphonomy: the anoxic, cold, dark burial that preserved the card DNA where the depot’s refrigerated holdings rotted on the ordinary schedule. The genetics are the period’s own established results, restated: the effective-size thresholds near fifty and five hundred, the loss of heterozygosity at about one part in twice the effective size per generation, runs of homozygosity as an inbreeding-coefficient estimator, mitochondrial haplotype diversity as a maternal tracer, and the tested room-temperature stability of the cards. The depot’s official name is on the ledger’s missing cover and nowhere else in the recovered pages; the Faculty refers to the holding by the doab and by the cards themselves. What the sampling was, and whether the shelf speaks for the landrace or only for the road, are not in the record and are not reconstructed here.
