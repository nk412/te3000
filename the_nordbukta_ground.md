# The Nordbukta Ground

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from the travel-time and noise-rejection logs of a cabled acoustic thermometry array and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Nordbukta Ground is the name, recovered from a single engraved titanium plate, of a Late Combustion acoustic thermometry array in the Fram Strait and Greenland Sea whose automatic logs run unbroken for forty-five years across the onset of the Contraction.** Travel times, gating log and watch notes are record; temperatures, mixed-layer depths and vessel tracks are the Faculty's inversions.

---

**The Nordbukta Ground** was a permanent, automated network of low-frequency sound sources and receivers moored across the Fram Strait sill and over the Greenland Sea gyre, deployed in +96 pT (2041 CE) to track the Atlantic inflow to the Arctic and the gyre's winter convective chimney by the travel time of coded sound pulses. It transmitted every three hours from deployment until +141 pT (2086 CE), logging by cable to a shore node whose archive is the sole surviving copy. Its project designation is lost. Its noise-rejection subsystem, built only to keep ship noise out of the temperature signal, logged 61,212 vessel-noise events, the last twelve years of them the only surviving record of traffic through the strait in the Contraction's first decade. Whether those hulls were going somewhere or going missing, the log cannot say.

## Sound as thermometer

Sound speed in seawater rises with temperature, salinity and pressure, near 0 °C by about 4.6 m/s per degree. A pulse over a fixed path arrives earlier when the water is warmer, and travel time inverts to the column's heat content. In +49 pT (1994 CE) a transarctic experiment transmitted a coherent 19.6 Hz tone at 195 dB, roughly 251 W of acoustic power, from a source north of Svalbard to receivers 1,000 km and 2,600 km away, and resolved a warming of about 0.4 °C in the Atlantic-origin layer beneath the Arctic surface. A four-mooring follow-on in the Fram Strait in +65–+67 pT (2010–2012 CE) showed the measurement could be kept up from moored hardware through the ice season. The Nordbukta Ground was their permanent descendant, built by an institute whose name survives only as a key into a registry that does not.

## Seven stations

Four stations lay along the Fram Strait sill, powered and read through a seabed cable to a shore node on the Svalbard side; three were battery moorings over the gyre centre near 75° N, on the ground the plate names. Sources transmitted a twenty-minute coded sequence centred on 40 Hz every three hours; receivers correlated against the code and logged arrivals to a tenth of a millisecond, each clock disciplined to the timing broadcast of the positioning constellation. Over the 280 km path from sill to gyre, one hundredth of a degree moves the arrival by about six milliseconds.

The band it used is the band ships make. Propeller cavitation and machinery tonals fall between roughly 10 and 300 Hz, and a hull inside the array's aperture could bury the coherent arrival by tens of decibels. The receivers therefore ran a gate: when in-band energy exceeded threshold for more than thirty seconds without correlating against the code, the window was discarded and a line was written recording station, time, duration, band energy and the bearing walk from paired hydrophones. The gate existed to protect the thermometer. Its log was never meant to be read as anything.

## The cap and the ceiling

Deep convection in the Greenland Sea requires two things: a preconditioned column, with weak stratification and dense water domed toward the surface by the cyclonic gyre, and a winter buoyancy loss at the surface large enough to overcome whatever stratification remains. The second condition had long been assisted by a recurring winter tongue of ice, the Odden, which rejected brine into the surface layer as it froze; through +45–+55 pT (1990–2000 CE) the ice edge retreated westward and the tongue came less and less often. The annual maximum mixed-layer depth peaked around +68 pT (2013 CE), then fell by about half, from a +63–+69 pT (2008–2014 CE) average near 1,168 m to a +70–+75 pT (2015–2020 CE) average near 559 m. The age's literature called the column capped, at length.

A scheme in the shore node's files from +105 pT (2050 CE) proposed supplying the second condition by force: hulls cutting leads across the gyre, exposing open water to the coldest air. The array's own record was used to reject it. Over open water at its freezing point under the coldest available Arctic air, turbulent and radiative heat loss to the atmosphere peaks near 700–800 W/m²; the figure is set by the exchange between water and air, and nothing floating on the water raises it. A lead 40 m wide across a gyre 300 km across exposes that ceiling over about one part in seven thousand of the surface. The proposal is not mentioned again.

Thirty-one winters of shallow convection follow, the mixed layer inverting to 300–700 m. In the winter of +128 pT (2073 CE), with the gyre moorings on their last cells, the path across the gyre centre returned a travel-time anomaly that inverts to a homogeneous column deeper than 1,100 m, the deepest since +68 pT (2013 CE). The inversion is the Faculty's, from a single path. No annotation in the archive refers to it.

## What the gate kept

Vessel-noise events run at 1,300–1,900 per year through the Late Combustion. From +129 pT (2074 CE) the count rises, to 5,100 in +136 pT (2081 CE), and the signatures change: more single-screw tonals, lower source levels, more events in the dark months, and bearing walks that resolve at the sill overwhelmingly to northbound and eastbound passage. Okonkwo, D., treating the gating log as an unintended census, clustered the events into 11,400 distinct tracks across the last twelve years of the record.

The array's aperture ended at its outermost hydrophone. A hull that passed the sill and went on to a harbour, and a hull that passed the sill and went under, wrote the same next line, which is none. Ranganathan, V., notes that a sail or a quiet electric drive left nothing, so the census counts noisy engines. Zhou-Ferreira, A., reads the rise as ordinary redistribution of fishery and passage traffic toward an opening strait. Lindqvist-Adeyemi, R., who first published the log, declines to convert tracks to persons; the array cannot tell forty aboard from four.

## Watch notes and silence

Each daily bundle carried a free-text watch note. Until +121 pT (2066 CE) a header flag marked whether a duty operator or the node's summarising system, trained on the operators' own notes, had written it; a format migration that year dropped the header and the flag with it. From that bundle to the last, the notes are unclassifiable. A representative line from +133 pT (2078 CE):

> `STN 3 arrival +41 ms vs 30 d mean. 6 gated, 30–80 Hz, longest 2 h 14 min, bearing 190→010. Cap intact. No action.`

Whether a person or the summariser wrote "No action" cannot be determined, and the Faculty's stylometric tests return the same answer for every note after the migration.

The last service visit, in +119 pT (2064 CE), replaced the cells on two of the three gyre moorings and left the third. The gyre stations fell silent in +125, +126 and +131 pT (2070, 2071 and 2076 CE) as their cells ran down. The sill stations continued on cable power until the node's last bundle, dated +141 pT (2086 CE). The temperature record of the Atlantic inflow across the sill runs unbroken for the full forty-five years, the longest continuous physical measurement the Faculty holds from either side of the Contraction's onset.

## Provenance and ground

The shore node wrote its bundles to write-once optical cartridges kept in a rock-cut store in permafrost; Halvorsen, E., attributes their legibility over the interval to temperature alone. The station 2 pressure case, a titanium alloy whose passive oxide film Halvorsen rates as effectively immune to seawater, was recovered from the gyre ground with its plate legible: `NORDBUKTA GROUND · STN 2 · S/R · IF RECOVERED RETURN TO OWNER`. Whether *Nordbukta* named the project, the mooring ground or the owner's fjord is unresolved; every digital file refers to the array by a numeric registry key.
