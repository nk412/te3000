# The Step-Aside Gate

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a market-hall entry ledger kept by the gate itself and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Step-Aside Gate is the Faculty's gloss for the lost name of an automated fever-screening gate at a grain-market hall on the Indo-Gangetic plain, whose entry ledger was for some centuries read as the case curve of a lost epidemic.** The ledger and the gate's posted instruction are record; the reading of the refusals as disease, and its correction, are reconstruction.

---

**The Step-Aside Gate was not a plague record, and the epidemic it was once taken to document did not occur in the ledger's data.** It was an infrared forehead-temperature turnstile that admitted a hall-licence holder or refused them at a fixed threshold, and it logged each decision as a transaction. Its refusals rose through eleven summers, from +141 pT (2086 CE) to +152 pT (2097 CE), and the early catalogue of the hall's ledger entered them as a contagion of rising incidence. They were a thermometer reading the weather.

## The first reading

The posted card, recovered from the gate housing, reads: `LOOK AT THE LIGHT. ABOVE 37.5 — STEP ASIDE. WAIT FIFTEEN MINUTES. ASK AGAIN.` The ledger logs each pass as `ADMIT` or `ASIDE`. In the cool months `ASIDE` stands near 2% of entries. By the hot months it reaches 61% of afternoon entries, and the early catalogue read the climb as an outbreak, assigned it a mortality, and gave it a name.

## The correction

Three features of the ledger fail the reading. First, an infection does not leave at night: refusals peak between 14:00 and 16:00 and fall to near nothing before dawn, daily. Second, a contagion has a shape. Under any ordinary transmission model with a basic reproduction number near two and a serial interval of several days, incidence climbs, crests, and falls as susceptible people are exhausted; this curve plateaus, and the same June crest returns in each of the eleven years. Third, the same entrants are refused, wait the fifteen minutes the card prescribes, and are refused again.

The cause is physical. A forehead's surface temperature follows the air around it; as ambient wet-bulb temperature approaches 35 °C, the skin cannot shed heat to the air and converges on core temperature. The threshold was set for a cool, dry plain and carried no ambient compensation. At a low true prevalence, a screening test of ordinary specificity returns mostly false positives; at 0.1% prevalence, a sensitivity of 0.7 and specificity of 0.95 give a positive predictive value near 1.4%. The gate measured exactly what it was built to measure, to the tolerance it was built to, and the world beneath it moved out of that tolerance.

## Final pages

The last recovered page is dated +152 pT (2097 CE). It records the day's 3,100 entry attempts as `ASIDE`, the stall-keeper's own licence token among them.

---

## Provenance and ground

The ledger was recovered as unencrypted telemetry from a sensor-vendor mirror. The posted card is the sole inscription; the gate's firmware does not survive.
