The Moyynkum Teeth

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a skeletal assemblage of thirty-one individuals and the pathogen genome recovered from their dental pulp, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

The Moyynkum Teeth are a burial assemblage of the recovery centuries, from a well-line holding on the Central Asian sand-steppe, whose tooth chambers carry in nine individuals the genome of the flea-borne bacterium that had been circulating in the burrows under that ground since long before the Combustion Age. The bones and the genomes are record; the holding, its water, and the season are reconstruction.

In the autumn of +561 pT (2506 CE) a holding on the Moyynkum sands buried thirty-one of its people in one row, all within a few weeks of each other by the state of the graves. The agent is recovered by function only: a bacterium of rodent burrows, carried by flea, which after an incubation of one to seven days kills, untreated, in something between two-fifths and two-thirds of cases. It had never gone anywhere. What had gone was the count that used to say when it was coming.

Under the sand

The reservoir is a metapopulation. Great gerbils live in family groups of about ten in permanent, defended burrow systems scattered across the desert; each occupied system is a subpopulation, and the bacterium crosses between them only as fleas do, on animals moving from one to the next. That geometry makes the focus a percolation problem. Below about a third of burrow systems occupied, the occupied ones form no cluster spanning the landscape and an infection burns out locally; above it, a connected path exists and the epizootic crosses the whole focus. Occupancy rises with the water: wet summers feed the vegetation, the gerbils breed, the fraction climbs. A flea whose foregut a bacterial biofilm has plugged starves, feeds repeatedly, refluxes into the bite, and transmits at perhaps a quarter to a half of each attempt before dying within days. Human cases are spill from an epizootic already running.

The count that was kept

Through the middle Combustion Age a continental monitoring service walked autumn transects across these sands, scoring each burrow system occupied or empty by its fresh diggings, combing fleas from trapped animals, returning one fraction per sector. The fraction was the whole instrument, and it gave two years’ warning. The routine survives bit-exact in the Code Deposit, provably original by the fingerprint the period’s repositories gave every file:

OCC_CRIT = 0.33       # spanning cluster; below this the focus is disconnected
for s in sectors:
    if occ[s][t] > OCC_CRIT: warn(s, year=t + 2)

The same decades published the climate term: a spring one degree warmer raises prevalence in the reservoir by more than half. Both findings were in print by +63 pT (2008 CE); the occupancy series that fed them were held on media rated for ten to thirty years and were not recopied. No sector’s count survives. The rule survives without its input, which is the usual shape of the loss.

What the pulp carries

The lineage in the nine pulp chambers falls inside the focus’s own deep clade, beside no imported branch. At roughly one fixed substitution per twenty years across a 4.6-megabase genome the clock places it to within a generation and no finer, but it excludes arrival from outside: the people were new, the bacterium was not. Their own genomes, from the same teeth, show runs of homozygosity shortening across the three generations before the burial, in a pattern following the corridor they came up rather than any rule of descent — the movement inland and upland off ground the heat and the wells had settled. They came to the water that was left, and the water stood on top of the focus. The sands have not been counted since.

Provenance and ground

Dry saline sand and a sealed pulp chamber are the standard conditions for pathogen DNA at this depth of time; the taphonomy is Halvorsen’s. Radiocarbon remains unusable across the interval. Sarpong-Vidal, R. (2954) placed the lineage and reads the assemblage as spill from a percolating epizootic; a single burrow struck while digging is not excluded by nine genomes.
