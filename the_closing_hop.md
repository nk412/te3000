# The Closing Hop

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from the genomes and skeletons of nine adults buried in a row beneath a mast anchor, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Closing Hop was the last link of a five-station radio time-signal chain, run at about 52° N in the −20s pT, that was meant to bring one clock back to its own starting reading after a circuit of the Earth.** The nine burials and one film reel of station log are record; the chain, its purpose, and the staffing of its last station are reconstruction.

---

**The Closing Hop** is the Faculty's name for the fifth transmission of a chain that passed a time signal eastward through four stations and then back to the first. Its own name for the practice is lost; the log's recurring heading is rendered "the keeping-together." The chain ran from −21 pT (1924 CE) to −9 pT (1936 CE). Its last station stood beside a detention camp for people moved on from three regions, and its operators were drawn from the camp.

## The loop

Each station set its clock from the signal of the one before and sent on its own reading. Five stations on one parallel enclose a loop whose light-time is the circumference over *c*: at 52° N, about 24,670 km, or 82 ms. Light-signal synchronisation is consistent hop to hop but not around a rotating loop. The residual is 2Ω*A*/*c*², where Ω is the Earth's rotation rate and *A* the enclosed area; on the equator it is 207 ns, and at 52° N it is that figure times cos²52°, about 79 ns, with sign set by direction of travel. The operators' chronograph printed to 1 ms. The shortfall is one part in 12,700 of their finest interval. Synchronisation by exchanged signals was the convention that made distant clocks agree at all, and it is the convention that cannot be made to close on the Earth that carried the stations. No instrument of the period could see this, and the log reports every circuit closed.

## The log

The reel, from an upper rack of the Longyear Deposit, holds 214 pages. A representative exchange, as transcribed:

> *Fifth to first: ours reads yours plus 0.000. Does it close.*
> *First to fifth: it closes.*
> *Fifth to first: then we are kept.*

## The assemblage

Nine adults lay in a row along a mast anchor, undated by any burial good. Enamel strontium ratios place their childhoods on three distinct geologies, none the local one. The genomes show three unrelated lineages and no first-degree kin. Forearm entheses are marked in a pattern consistent with sustained keying. Ibarra, N., takes the station as single-filament infrastructure at the scale of a camp; the operators' absence from the camp's own ration books is, on the Faculty's reading, unexplained.

## Provenance and ground

The reel and the burials are held together only by the mast anchor named on both.
