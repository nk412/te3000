# The Beacon Hearth

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a single silver-gelatin print found loose in a Consolidation-era source-retrieval register, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Beacon Hearth is the Historical Faculty's name for a winter camp of the early Consolidation, known from one photograph, in which a party on the road warmed a beacon's service hut with the strontium heat source that had powered the beacon.** The print, the plate it resolves, and the decay arithmetic are record; who held the camera, and what became of the four people whose bedding the print shows, are not.

---

**The print is a silver-gelatin contact photograph, 88 by 126 millimetres, of the inside of a stone service hut.** On the flat stone where a stove would stand sits a welded steel cylinder the size of a large tin: the heat-source capsule of a radioisotope beacon generator, out of its shield. Four sleeping places are laid around it, the nearest within a metre. Through the open door, on its side in the snow, lies the shield housing, lid off, a painted survey line across it. Nobody is in the frame. The hut stood on an upland pass of a poleward route of the Consolidation; the name of the pass, the beacon, and the party is unrecoverable. The Faculty can state the dose at each bed; it cannot state whether the beds are empty because the party moved on or because it did not.

## The plate

The generator was of a type the age built by the thousand for unattended lights — single-filament, in Ibarra's sense: a few kilograms of strontium-90 as ceramic titanate, double-sealed in welded steel, in a shield of depleted uranium and lead weighing several hundred kilograms, yielding ten watts of electricity from two hundred and thirty of heat. The plate reads, as the print renders it:

> HEAT SOURCE Sr-90 · 1 480 TBq AT CHARGE · CHARGED 2076-03 · DO NOT REMOVE FROM SHIELD · UNSHIELDED, LETHAL AT ONE METRE WITHIN HOURS · RETURN TO [illegible]

The housing outside is painted *SURVEYED 2113 · INTACT · LISTED FOR RECOVERY*. The unit was charged in +131 pT (2076 CE); the survey listed it in +168 pT (2113 CE); the photograph was taken in the winter of +213 pT (2158 CE), forty-five years after that. The shield is the part of the unit that could not be carried. The capsule is the part that could.

## The arithmetic of the hut

Strontium-90 halves every 28.8 years; its daughter yttrium-90, half-life 64 hours, follows it in equilibrium, and between them they deliver about 1.13 MeV of beta energy per decay. Eighty-two years from charge is 2.85 half-lives: on the day of the photograph the capsule held about two hundred terabecquerels and gave some thirty watts of heat, enough to hold its surface tens of degrees above the hut — too hot to keep a hand on, not hot enough to mark one. That is the warmth the beds were laid for. The steel stops the beta particles; what leaves is bremsstrahlung, X-rays thrown off by stopping electrons, which the period's rule of thumb puts in iron at one part in fifty of the beta power: half a watt of penetrating radiation, of the order of half a gray an hour at a metre. At the nearest bed a night's sleep books about four grays, the whole-body dose at which roughly half of untreated adults die within two months; at the farthest, near a gray a night. The skin turned toward the capsule takes more: reddening within days, lifting wet past fifteen to twenty grays, necrosis past thirty-five. The hut is warm. At the distances the print shows, the camp has a life measured in nights.

## Who held the camera

The print was found tipped loose into the back board of a source-retrieval register kept by an upland successor authority in the +220s pT (2160s CE), which lists recovered units by plate number; the plate in the photograph is not in it. Two readings hold. On the first, one of the party made the photograph and carried it out: the party moved on, and the print is evidence that someone left the hut well enough to walk. On the second, the register's surveyors made it on finding the hut as shown, beds laid and capsule warm, and left the capsule for want of a shield to carry it in — and the beds are laid because the people who laid them did not get up. The print gives no one's face and no one's absence. The first school reads it as proof that someone walked out; the second as a survey of a hut nobody walked out of. Both fit the frame.

## Provenance and ground

The witness is one object: the print, fibre-based silver-gelatin, kept flat, dry, and dark between the boards of a register for eight centuries — the survival mode Halvorsen, E. classes with the bound-and-forgotten. Its date is the verso inscription, in a Consolidation regional count that Ashworth-Nakamura, K. converts to the winter of +213 pT (2158 CE) within a year. The plate, the painted line, and the bed distances are read from the emulsion at grain scale. The dose figures are derived, not measured. The register contributes its find context and one negative fact — the plate number's absence — and nothing about the people.

## Notes

1. Dating follows the Faculty convention, CE = pT + 1945. The half-lives and beta energies cited are the period's own measured values. The bremsstrahlung fraction is the period's thick-target approximation — atomic number times maximum beta energy in MeV, over three thousand — and the dose rate at a metre derived from it is quoted to its order of magnitude only.
2. The plate's hazard line is the period's own case for this class of unit, stamped on the capsule because the case had already occurred; the survey tag records that a retrieval was scheduled. Nothing in the record says why it did not happen, and the Faculty does not supply a reason.
