The Steady-State Line

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from oral-tradition residue in colloquial Standard Terran and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

The Steady-State Line is the Faculty’s designation for an unnamed Middle Combustion Age ammonium-nitrate concentration plant whose thermal runaway survives only as a fixed idiom in the living language. Idiom, isotope horizon, and a genetic pattern are record; plant and sequence are reconstruction.

On 3 March 1971 CE (+26 pT) a hold vessel of hot concentrated ammonium-nitrate solution at a fertiliser works decomposed faster than its cooling jacket could remove the heat, and the vessel and its building ceased to exist. The plant’s name does not survive in any form; the designation is the Faculty’s, not recovered.

The loop

Ammonium nitrate decomposes exothermically, and the rate rises exponentially with temperature in the Arrhenius manner; a cooling jacket removes heat only in proportion to the melt–coolant gap, up to the pump’s capacity. Below a critical temperature the two curves cross and the vessel holds; above it they do not, and no quantity of coolant restores the balance. The plant held its vessel at 130 °C under one automated rule, unchanged for an estimated eleven years: open the coolant valve in proportion to the excess over setpoint. A later Faculty chemical reconstruction blames chloride in a changed process-water feed, which catalyses the decomposition and lowers its threshold; no assay survives. The readings that day were the readings the loop had always seen, meaning something else; it executed its rule correctly, drove the valve to its mechanical stop, and continued to report that it had. One log line survives as a spoken formula:

03-03 14:47 TIC-204 PV 137.4 SP 130.0 OUT 100% ALM NONE

The idiom full valve and climbing, in general use for an effort at its maximum that is not sufficient, is the entry’s fragment. Its speakers do not, on the Faculty’s sampling, know that it refers to a valve.

What is left

Ammonium nitrate is highly water-soluble and its solutions attack ordinary carbon steel; the residues washed out within seasons and the structural metal, standing at the water table, oxidised away. Halvorsen, E., reports no diagnostic metal, no vessel footprint, and soil nitrate indistinguishable from any farmed ground of the period. A laminated pond core 1.2 km downslope carries a negative δ¹⁵N excursion of about two per mil in one horizon, consistent with a pulse of factory-fixed nitrogen, dated to the tradition’s year.

A mill town 4 km away had shown tight local endogamy for centuries. That pattern loosens sharply within one to two generations of +26 pT. The survey cannot separate the plant from any other cause of dispersal in those decades; the Faculty records it as suggestive, not attribution.

Provenance and ground

The idiom is the primary anchor, dated by its control vocabulary and the sediment horizon; setpoint, tag number, and service life are reconstructed. Halvorsen, E., Taphonomy of soluble-process sites, supplies the site reading. No plant records, casualty figures, or period accounts are known.
