The Angel Return

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a coastal early-warning radar’s clutter-rejection log, kept to tune the receiver rather than to count birds, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

The Angel Return is the Historical Faculty’s name for a coastal air-defence radar’s biological-echo counts, the only surviving continuous census of a seabird colony the installation was never built to watch. The gated counts are physical record; the hand that kept annotating them past what the form required is recovered only by function, unlinked to any name the station itself preserves.

At 61°02′ N, on a wave-cut stack off a North Atlantic shelf, an automated early-warning radar stood watch from +14 pT (1959 CE), gated from +16 pT (1961 CE) to reject “angels” — the operators’ term for the wingbeat-modulated returns of birds — logging a count each watch to hold its own false-alarm threshold, not to survey wildlife. It thereby kept the only continuous census of the kittiwake (Rissa tridactyla) colony on the stack below.

The ceiling

A kittiwake’s chick-provisioning range is fixed by flight energetics to roughly 45 km, enclosing shelf sea sufficient, on contemporary prey-density surveys, for on the order of eleven thousand pairs. The counts crossed that ceiling in +30 pT (1975 CE), climbed four seasons more on the lag of already-fledged chicks, then fell by nine-tenths in three, once a nearby fishery’s automated catch quota — set against a season-old stock assessment — took the sandeel biomass under it. The discriminator needed no retuning to keep working: it logged flawlessly, a zero count every watch, for eleven more years after the last recorded return. One entry carries a hand not on the form:

0620Z ANGEL CT 000 — third season running. someone should look at the stack.

Provenance and ground

Recovered from the station’s own clutter-rejection archive; Okonkwo (2913) first read the counts as a census rather than noise. The foundation course carries three names cut in +14 pT by the crew that poured it; none matches the marginal hand with any confidence, so the annotator is recovered here only by function.
