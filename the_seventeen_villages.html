The Seventeen Villages

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from the exterior serial plate and case architecture of a single intact canal-gate telemetry unit recovered from the Aralkum salt crust, cross-read against a matching telemetry slice held in the Wasatch Corpus, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

The Seventeen Villages is the Historical Faculty’s name for a single intact gate-control unit whose internal audit memory was irreversibly erased at the moment of its last human contact. The unit’s case, its serial plate, and six years of its routine operating telemetry are record; the allocation decisions its erased memory once held are, and by the age’s own mathematics will remain, unrecoverable.

The object itself survives whole. It is a cast-aluminium enclosure the size of a large filing drawer, bolted since +5 pT (1950 CE) to the head of the extension’s regulating gate on the lower reach of a feeder canal off the Aral basin’s eastern delta, and it still bears, legible under decades of salt varnish, the dedication plate cast into the structure at completion: seventeen settlement names in two columns, the canal’s declared beneficiaries, under a single word the Faculty renders untranslated for want of a better one. Nothing about the plate required decoding. What defeated every reading that has been attempted since is the smaller, later object bolted inside it — a tamper-evident control and logging module retrofitted to the gate in +134 pT (2079 CE), whose case is undamaged, whose flash memory is physically intact and fully populated with ciphertext, and whose contents are, on the surviving evidence, permanently and provably beyond recovery. The gate remembers everything it was told to forget nothing about. It can no longer say any of it.

The gate and the treaty

By the time of the retrofit the basin’s irrigated command area had been contracting for more than a century, on a trend already well attested in the age’s own surviving cartography and confirmed independently by satellite-return archives from well before the Attrition: the inland sea the canal had once helped to drain was, by the Late Combustion Age, already reduced to a fraction of its Middle Combustion extent, and the extension’s seventeen founding villages had by +130 pT (2075 CE) fallen to six still drawing water at the gate. The remaining flow, shared among fewer users, became the subject of a formal interbasin adjudication that required, as a condition of continued allocation, a tamper-evident record of exactly how the gate divided what arrived. The retrofit was the compliance answer: a locally generated symmetric key, a real-time clock, a set of flow and gate-position sensors, and an audit store that recorded every operator action against the schedule the treaty had set.

Ibarra, N., whose standing catalogue of the age’s single-filament infrastructure already named, without specifying them, one canal, one ore body, and the two poultry bloodlines onto which entire supply chains had narrowed, is read by the current consensus as having meant this gate by “one canal”: by +140 pT (2085 CE) it was the only surviving offtake serving any of the six villages, so that a single tamper switch, a single battery, and a single key stood between the adjudication record and total silence. No redundant unit was ever installed. None of the treaty’s signatories is recorded as having asked for one.

Two logs, one gate

The retrofit kept two records, and only one of them left the site. A leased telemetry circuit, billed by the byte to the adjudication authority, carried the gate’s scheduled setpoint — the fraction of flow nominally due each of the six offtakes on the rotating cycle the treaty specified — to a regional monitoring desk at intervals of six hours; this stream, unencrypted because the setpoint alone was judged too routine to warrant the additional circuit cost of ciphering it, is the slice later swept up, along with a great deal else on the same leased backbone, into what the discipline now catalogues as the Wasatch Corpus. Any manual departure from the schedule — an operator holding an offtake shut, or open, against the nominal rotation, which the retrofit’s own procedure required whenever total flow fell short of the sum the schedule promised — was logged only to the unit’s local encrypted store, because the override reason code was judged too sensitive, and too large, to carry on the same billed link.

The consequence is landed here without further comment because the record does not permit one: a gate spending its final three operating years holding one offtake in every four cycles shut against the published schedule would generate, on the surviving telemetry alone, a setpoint stream indistinguishable from a gate rotating flow evenly among all six villages every cycle without exception. An early reading of the Wasatch slice took the six-year rotation at face value, as evidence that the basin’s terminal allocation had been managed without favour to the end. The reading did not survive scrutiny of the circuit’s own billing schedule, which shows the override channel priced and provisioned from the retrofit’s first year — proof that overrides were expected, budgeted for, and never transmitted.

The battery

The gate kept local power through a lead-acid cell trickle-charged from a small photovoltaic panel, sized to run the controller and its tamper mesh for years after the regional grid supplying the rest of the retrofit’s telemetry link had become intermittent. By +151 pT (2096 CE) the panel was, by a wide margin, the only reliable source of stored electrical capacity within a day’s walk of the gate. The unit’s own final log entry — the last bit pattern its key ever encrypted before the key ceased to exist — records a case-intrusion event at 03:41 local time on a date the Faculty gives only in pT, followed within four seconds by the controller’s own zeroisation routine clearing the key from its battery-backed memory exactly as its manufacturer specified it would. Halvorsen, E.’s taphonomic reading, based on tool marks at the case seam consistent with hand tools rather than the retrofit’s own maintenance kit, holds that whoever opened the case that night wanted the battery, not the record; nothing else of value to a person standing at the gate in +151 pT lived inside it. The tamper mesh did exactly what it was built to do. It has no way to know, and the retrofit’s designers had no reason to specify, that what it was protecting was, by then, wanted for a different reason than the one it was guarding against.

The arithmetic of the seal

The key the mesh erased was 256 bits, generated on-device from a hardware entropy source and never escrowed, transmitted, or written to any second medium — a deliberate design choice, since an escrowed key would have let the very party accused of favouring one offtake over another produce a record of its own innocence on demand, defeating the treaty’s purpose. This is the reasoning the age is on record as having applied to its own audit hardware generally, and it is the reasoning that now makes the archive’s contents unrecoverable in the same act that once made them trustworthy: a record no one but the gate itself could alter is, once the gate forgets, a record no one can read either.

The unreadability is not a claim about present-day effort. It is bounded below by a physical constant, Landauer’s principle, verified experimentally within the Combustion Age itself: erasing one bit of information at temperature T costs at least kT ln 2 of dissipated energy, where k is the Boltzmann constant. Treating each of the key’s 2²⁵⁶ ≈ 1.16 × 10⁷⁷ candidate values as needing only a single bit-erasure to test — a assumption far more generous to an attacker than any real search, which must compare a whole trial decryption against the ciphertext — sets a floor under any exhaustive search, however it is carried out, on whatever hardware, at whatever point in the future it is attempted:

Search environment Minimum energy for 2²⁵⁶ single-bit tests Compare
Ordinary ambient temperature, 300 K ≈ 3.3 × 10⁵⁶ J —
Coldest possible computer, at the cosmic microwave background, 2.725 K ≈ 3.0 × 10⁵⁴ J —
Total energy radiated by a Sun-like star over its full ≈10-billion-year main-sequence lifetime ≈ 1.2 × 10⁴⁴ J ten orders of magnitude short of the coldest-computer floor

No refinement of method changes this by a figure large enough to matter; it is a bound on physics, not on cleverness. Nor does the age’s later development of large-scale quantum computation help, and the reason is the second half of the argument, not a repetition of the first: quantum search of an unstructured space (Grover’s algorithm) gives only a quadratic speed-up, reducing a 256-bit key’s effective search cost to that of a 128-bit key — itself still many orders of magnitude beyond the table above — whereas the algorithm that did eventually open the Wasatch Corpus’s own traffic (Shor’s algorithm, against the public-key exchange that traffic’s transport layer depended on) has no target here at all, because a symmetric key generated from noise and never exchanged over any channel was never secured by a factoring or discrete-logarithm problem in the first place. The Wasatch Corpus’s cryptography failed because a hard problem later turned out to have a shortcut. This unit’s cryptography cannot fail in that way, because there is no problem left to solve: the quantity that would have to be found no longer has any physical instantiation anywhere for any process to act on.

The seventeen

What the erased log would have shown is which of the six remaining villages held its share and which did not, and for how long, across six years of a shortage the treaty’s own schedule assumed would not occur. The genomic tell offers a proxy, not an answer. Endogamy coefficients among descendant lineages traced to the extension’s six late-era villages relax unevenly across the interval the gate was operating, in a pattern that tracks the routes later confirmed, from resettlement genetics and dental strontium elsewhere in the basin, as the ones people actually left by — not the routes any surviving administrative record describes them as having been assigned. Whether the households that moved earliest did so because the gate had already stopped serving them, or moved for reasons the gate’s schedule never touched at all, is exactly the distinction the erased override log existed to make and exactly the distinction no other surviving instrument can.

Two readings hold the field without resolving each other. One takes the pre-shortage rotation, visible and even in the Wasatch telemetry for the retrofit’s first six years, as the baseline the override log most likely continued under duress, since nothing in the treaty’s design incentivised the operators to deviate from a schedule they had helped negotiate. The other notes that the treaty’s own override procedure was invoked, by the billing record’s own count, on a rising fraction of cycles from +146 pT (2091 CE) onward, and that a procedure invoked more with each passing year is not one a reading of the earlier, unstressed years can safely be extended to cover. Neither reading is falsifiable against anything the gate itself can still be made to say.

Provenance and ground

The unit’s exterior — case, dedication plate, and mounting hardware — was surveyed in place by an Institute for Deep Record Studies field team and is catalogued, on the evidence of its internal architecture and the confirmed futility of every recovery attempt made against it, among the Sealed. Its stamped serial number, unaffected by the internal zeroisation, was matched to a telemetry stream in the Wasatch Corpus bearing the same identifier, giving the six years of setpoint history that frame this entry; the billing ledger for the retrofit’s leased circuit, held separately, supplied the override-frequency count. Okonkwo, D.’s method for reading incidental metadata as unintended census was applied to the billing record, not to the telemetry itself, on the working principle that a circuit’s invoice is one layer further from anyone’s intent to be believed than the traffic it carried.

Notes

The extension’s original seventeen place-names, legible on the exterior plate, are not repeated here; six had current occupants at the time of the retrofit, and which six is a matter of settlement record independent of this entry. No survey since +151 pT has recovered any second copy of the unit’s key, nor is one expected. The Institute’s standing instruction is to re-inspect the case at each centennial survey for evidence of any register the original zeroisation routine did not reach. None has been found so far.
