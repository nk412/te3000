# The Fixed Share

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a hoard of struck brass tokens, a paper subscription roll, and nineteen sealed catalyst drums recovered from a dry gallery of a spent coal working above the Río Turbio, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Fixed Share is the Historical Faculty's name for the brass-token allocation system of a wind-powered nitrogen works on the far southern Patagonian steppe, and for the operator community — the Fijadores of the token legend — who kept a Contraction-era resettlement district supplied with fixed nitrogen for at least forty-five years.** The tokens, the subscription roll, and the drums are record; the works itself, never located on the ground, and the shape of the crews' working lives are reconstruction from what those three classes of object can bear.

---

Nine hundred metres into a worked-out sub-bituminous coal seam at 51°32′ S, 72°18′ W, in the valley of the Río Turbio at the southern rim of the Patagonian steppe, a dry side gallery holds the entire recovered archive of a nitrogen works that has never itself been found. The deposit comprises two hundred and fourteen struck brass tokens and the two steel dies that struck them; a paper subscription roll of sixty-one leaves, kept legible by nine centuries of cold, dry, dark air; and nineteen drums of promoted iron synthesis catalyst, sealed under nitrogen, their stencils intact. From these the Faculty reconstructs a small ammonia-synthesis plant established in +141 pT (2086 CE), fed by wind-driven electrolysis of river water, operated in continuous watches by a crew that struck its own name — FIJADORES, "the fixers" — into every token it issued, and distributing its product across a district of resettled upland farms in shares denominated in quintals of ammonia water. The last dated object in the gallery is a consignment note of +186 pT (2131 CE). This entry argues, following Ibarra, that the works is the clearest recovered instance of the age's most centralised chemistry surviving the Contraction by becoming small — a single filament of the old infrastructure deliberately re-spun at district scale — and that its roll, read closely, records who the new thread reached and who it measurably did not.

## Half the atoms

The works inherited a dependency older than the calendar. Industrial nitrogen fixation, established at scale from −32 pT (1913 CE), had by the Late Combustion Age worked itself into the species' own tissue: by the period's published estimates, between two-fifths and one-half of the world's population depended on synthetic fertiliser for the nitrogen in their food, and roughly half the nitrogen atoms in a living human body had passed through a factory reactor. The fixing itself was done in plants of enormous unit size — single trains producing thousands of tonnes a day, fed by fossil methane, sited on coasts and pipelines — an arrangement Ibarra's school cites as a type case of *single-filament infrastructure*: a planetary food supply drawn through a few dozen pressure vessels. The period's own engineering literature, well before the Contraction, had worked out the alternative in detail — small synthesis loops fed by electrolytic hydrogen from wind or sunlight, sized to a district rather than a hemisphere — and had built demonstration plants, which remained demonstrations for as long as the pipelines held. The Río Turbio works is that literature made necessary. It is an extrapolation the age performed on itself: nothing in the recovered specification is beyond the practice of the +60s pT (2000s CE); what changed was that by the +130s the large filament no longer reached the far south, and the people did.

They came with the sorting. The district the roll describes — upland grazing country converted piecemeal to grain as the high-latitude season lengthened — filled through the +120s and +130s pT (2065–2085 CE) in the pattern the wealth gradient always took: capital first and driest. The roll's oldest full shares attach to nineteen consolidated land parcels assembled in those decades, holdings bought cheap and early, ahead of the main movement, by owners with the means to relocate at leisure. The labour that worked those parcels arrived later and otherwise. Soil that had carried sheep carries grain only if nitrogen is found for it, and by +141 pT (2086 CE) there was no longer a filament to buy it from. The works was capitalised, the Faculty infers from the share structure, by the nineteen parcels themselves.

## The wall

The chemistry the works ran is the period's best-studied reaction, and the reason it needs a works at all is a single number: the triple bond of molecular nitrogen, at 945 kilojoules per mole, is among the strongest in chemistry. Air is four-fifths feedstock, and the feedstock is nearly inert. The synthesis — one nitrogen and three hydrogens to two of ammonia — is exothermic by 92.4 kilojoules per mole of nitrogen consumed, so by van 't Hoff its equilibrium constant falls as temperature rises; run cold, the reaction would go nearly to completion, and does not go at all, because nothing breaks the bond. An iron surface breaks it — dissociative adsorption of nitrogen onto the catalyst is the rate-limiting step — but iron does useful kinetics only above roughly 400 °C, where the same equilibrium has collapsed to almost nothing at ordinary pressure. A catalyst does not move an equilibrium; it reaches the same wall sooner. The wall was there in −36 pT (1909 CE) at the first laboratory demonstration, it was there when the works lit its converter, and biology had stood before it for three billion years already — the bacterial enzyme that fixes nitrogen in soil pays about sixteen molecules of ATP for every molecule of nitrogen it splits, which is not a way around the bond but a way of paying for it.

The one lever the wall leaves is pressure. Four molecules of gas become two, so compression drives the equilibrium toward product: at 200 bar and 450 °C the equilibrium mixture is roughly one-quarter ammonia, and a bed run fast enough to matter converts about fifteen per cent of the gas per pass. The remainder is not waste; the loop cools the effluent until ammonia condenses out as liquid, and recycles the unreacted gas back across the bed, so that what the equilibrium refuses in one pass it grants over several. Everything recognisable in the recovered specification follows from this arithmetic. The catalyst in the gallery drums is the classical charge — fused magnetite promoted with alumina, potash, and lime, reduced in service to porous metallic iron — and the drums' rated service pressure and the roll's occasional operating marginalia are consistent with a loop in the range of 150 to 200 bar, the compromise the period had run for two centuries: pressure bought from steel and compression work, yield bought from pressure, and the wall unmoved at the end of it.

The works' innovation, such as it was, lay upstream. Its hydrogen came not from methane but from the river: electrolysis of water takes nine tonnes of water per tonne of hydrogen by simple stoichiometry, and a tonne of ammonia contains about 178 kilograms of hydrogen, so each tonne of product drew roughly a tonne and three-fifths from the Río Turbio — a negligible abstraction from even a small cold-country river. Its nitrogen came from the air by pressure-swing adsorption. Its energy came from the wind, and the far southern steppe is, on the period's own surveys, one of the windiest inhabited places on the planet, with turbine capacity factors above two-fifths — figures coastal Europe's machines never approached. Electrolytic ammonia cost the works between thirty-four and forty-three gigajoules of electricity per tonne against a thermodynamic floor near nineteen; at an output the Faculty estimates from the token record at two to three tonnes a day, the whole works ran on a few megawatts of nameplate wind. The number is worth pausing on. A planetary industry that had been reproached for consuming between one and two per cent of the world's energy had a district-sized version that a hillside of wind machines could carry.

## The watch below the wind

What the machinery could not tolerate was the wind's schedule, and this is where the workforce enters the record as a workforce. A synthesis loop wants to run steadily: the reaction's own heat, exchanged from effluent to feed, keeps the bed at temperature without external firing, but only while gas is moving; a bed allowed to cool costs days to bring back; and a catalyst cycled hot and cold sinters, losing the fine porous surface that is the whole of its activity. Worse, the reduced iron is destroyed by the very air it works beside — oxygen compounds in the feed poison it reversibly, sulfur permanently — and freshly reduced catalyst is pyrophoric, hot enough on contact with open air to glow. The drums in the gallery are stencilled REDUCIDO Y PASIVADO — NO ABRIR AL AIRE ("reduced and passivated — do not open to air"): charges shipped ready-reduced under a deliberately grown oxide skin a few nanometres thick, to be brought carefully to temperature under process gas by people who knew exactly what they were holding.

The reconstruction of the operating community rests on the tokens' date runs and the roll's labour entries. Tokens were struck yearly from +143 to +185 pT (2088–2130 CE), an unbroken sequence of forty-three years; Okonkwo's group, sequencing the die wear, finds no year in which striking stopped and no evidence the dies were ever recut — a continuity of issue that is hard to read as anything but continuity of product. The works therefore held its loop hot, its hydrogen buffered against calm days in storage the specification implies but the gallery does not preserve, and its converter fed, through more than four decades of subpolar winters, on a labour system the roll shows directly: watch upon watch, entered in day-wages. Iron charges in steady service last a decade and more, and the gallery's count is consistent with exactly this — nineteen drums delivered, sixteen empty and returned to store, the works evidently drawing replacement charges at the unhurried rate of a plant run well. The Faculty's taphonomists note that nothing in the deposit records a rupture, a fire, or a poisoning of the bed. What the deposit records is competence, renewed annually, in brass.

## The quintal and the roll

The token is a disc of brass, thirty-one millimetres, struck SINDICATO DE FIJADORES about a windmill-and-flask device, and on the reverse UN QUINTAL — AGUA AMONIACAL with the year: one metric quintal, one hundred kilograms, of ammonia water, the dissolved form in which the works shipped its product for injection into soil. At the strengths the period used for direct application, a quintal of solution carried some sixteen kilograms of elemental nitrogen; a hectare of grain wanted on the order of a hundred kilograms of nitrogen a year; and the annual issue the dies and date runs support — several tens of thousands of quintals — is thus sufficient, by the Faculty's own derivation, for a district on the order of eight thousand hectares. The roll is the token system's ledger and its census. Full shares — cuotas enteras — run down the first column against the nineteen founding parcels, unchanged in forty-five years except by inheritance marks. The second column is half-shares, and nearly every one carries the same annotation, in a succession of different hands: *media cuota — pagada en jornales* ("half share — paid in day-labour"). The people who tended the converter, hauled the ammonia carts, and stood the winter watches were paid, in large part, in claims on the nitrogen they fixed, worked into land they had reached late and on foot.

There is a third column, and it is struck through. Eleven leaves near the roll's end record applications from settlements of the Atlantic littoral, three hundred kilometres east — communities the sorting had left in place rather than moved. Each entry concludes *sin reparto*: no distribution. The Faculty reads the phrase as logistical rather than punitive — a works shipping dilute solution by cart has a hard economic radius, and the littoral lay beyond it — and the roll offers nothing to contradict that reading. It offers only the gradient itself, entered without comment in the works' own hand: whole quintals to those who had arrived first and driest, half-quintals in wages to those who arrived late, a struck-through column for those who had not arrived at all.

## Gallery nine

Why the archive sits in a coal mine is the deposit's one small irony, and the Faculty declines to sharpen it. The seam above the Río Turbio had been worked for the boilers of the old filament and was spent before the works was founded; its deep galleries offered what the surface of that latitude does not — constant cold, constant darkness, and air dry enough to hold paper — and the works used gallery nine as its store and, evidently, its registry. Survival here required no accident of shelving: the gallery lies above the workings' water table, the seam's acid drainage ran below it, and brass, steel, and cold paper simply waited. It is the second recovered case, after the Longyear Deposit itself, of the age's record persisting in the emptied seam of the fuel it had stopped burning.

The record ends without ending. The consignment note of +186 pT (2131 CE) is routine — one drum drawn, one returned — and is followed by nothing. No leaf of the roll is a closure; the dies were not defaced, as retired dies in the period's minting practice commonly were; the sixteenth empty drum was racked in sequence. Whether the works stopped that season, or ran on for years past its last delivery to the mine, or moved — its vessels carted north or south to a district that needed them more, its crews with them — the gallery cannot say, and no reading has been able to force it. The nineteenth drum still stands where the Fijadores racked it, stencils legible, tare and gross weights chalked and agreeing: a full charge, reduced and passivated, sealed under nitrogen against the air for nine hundred years. The Faculty has not opened it.

## Provenance and ground

The gallery-nine deposit was opened in 2967 CE during a stratigraphic survey of the Río Turbio workings and is held complete by the Institute for Deep Record Studies. Halvorsen's taphonomic assessment attributes the paper roll's survival to the gallery's stable cold and its position above both the water table and the seam's acid drainage — the same class of accident that, flooding differently, produced the Svalbard bias in the Longyear Deposit. The tokens and dies are the sole attestation of the name Fijadores; it appears on no other recovered medium. The works site itself has not been located, a fact the Faculty attributes to the routine Consolidation-era salvage of pressure steel and turbine towers rather than to any event.
