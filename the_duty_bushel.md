# The Duty Bushel

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a coal-wharf account book and the engine return it contradicts, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The duty bushel is the fuel unit of the Early Combustion Age's mine-engine performance returns, and this entry records why the efficiencies the Faculty derived from it stood a fifth too high for six centuries.** The wharf account and the return are record; which of their figures a clerk wrote is not.

---

The duty bushel was not a volume, and for most of its life not a weight anyone had put on a scale. It was the unit of account in which one south-western tin district published its mine engines' monthly performance — pounds of water raised one foot per bushel of coal — the bushel fixed at 94 lb after a survey of thirty-one works in −114 pT (1831 CE) had weighed it, on average, at 92.43. One return for an unnamed 80-inch pumping engine survives:

> *Oct. 1837. Coals, bush. of 94 lb, 1,164. Load 42,318 lb. Stroke 10 ft. Strokes 240,400. Duty 87,398,000.*

## The wharf against the return

At 30 MJ/kg that is 118 MJ of work from 1,279 MJ of coal: 9.3%. The Faculty's tables long carried the district's best returns at 13% and used them as the calibration point for the age's early energy budget. A boiler at the district's attested 40 psi (saturation 142 °C) against a 40 °C condenser has a Carnot bound of 24.5%, so the second-law screen the Faculty calibrated on these very returns passes every one of them. The works' wharf account lands about a fifth more coal than the return burns. Both survive in one Late Combustion transcription whose reader, trained on the legible folios, infilled the illegible ones — including three months after the engine is recorded stopped, which it filled at the district mean — and whose confidence column was removed in a commit titled *tidy*. The authorship void reaches back through the reader to −108 pT (1837 CE): the trial month is among the cells that cannot now be assigned to a clerk or to the machine. The tables still print 13%. Reckoned on the wharf's coal, October comes to 71,600,000.

## Provenance and ground

Both books survive only as one local-history dataset committed to a public repository and swept into consignment L-III of the Longyear Deposit, where the Svalbard bias kept it above the acid. Okonkwo (2961) dated the *tidy* commit from its own metadata; the wharf correction is Marchetti's.

## Notes

The return states no boiler pressure; 40 psi is the district's attested working figure. The 30 MJ/kg assumed for the wharf's coal is the middle of the bituminous range; the ratio, not the absolute, carries the correction.
