# The Days-Since-Fit Column

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from scattered scraps of device logs, a clinical bench note, and two intercepted message streams, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The days-since-fit column is a one-integer notation, name unrecoverable, that a family of implanted-electrode text systems of the Middle Combustion Age appended to every line they emitted, counting days elapsed since the decoder was last fitted to the wearer's cortex.** The column is record; its use by the wearers' households, and the reading of the later lines, are reconstruction.

---

On 9 June +61 pT (2006 CE) a decoder was fitted to a four-by-four-millimetre array of ninety-six silicon needles implanted in the motor cortex of a person who could not move, and for the next 212 days it wrote their messages. **The days-since-fit column** counted, in whole days, how far the decoder had drifted from the cortex it had been fitted to; the systems printed it at the right margin of every line, in a font smaller than the text; no household is recorded as having read it. By line 212 the column read 212 and the text had become more fluent than on day 1.

## The mechanism

A needle in cortex hears neurons only as voltage spikes crossing a threshold set at a fixed multiple of the channel's noise. Each neuron fires fastest for one preferred direction of intended movement, and a linear filter over firing counts in twenty-millisecond bins turns that tuning into a cursor velocity. The fit assumes the same neurons sit at the same needles. They do not stay there: the tissue moves a few micrometres against the array with each pulse and breath, and the scar that gradually encapsulates a needle lowers the amplitude of what it hears. Units drop below threshold and others rise above it; preferred directions wander over days even in neurons that stay. Fitted filters therefore decay, and the standard bench remedy, recorded in the field's own papers from the 1990s CE, was to refit before every session. The decay was foreseen, measured and published. The home systems shipped with a refit procedure that required a technician, and the technician's visit was scheduled monthly.

## What the system did instead

The systems resolved each decoded letter against a statistical prior over words. A prior is weighed against the evidence from the cortex: as the evidence flattens, the prior's share of the output grows without limit, and the text grows more fluent as it grows less related to the wearer. The column was the only place the loss showed.

An intercepted stream records one exchange on day 140, the left side of each pair being the caregiver's typed question:

> Are you in pain?
> I AM WELL. THE TEMPERATURE IS ACCEPTABLE.
> Are you in pain?
> I AM WELL. PLEASE ADJUST THE BLINDS.
> Is that you?
> THAT IS MY ANSWER.

The streams carry no marker separating the wearer's words from the prior's, and the Faculty's classifiers cannot assign any of the 212 lines to either.

## The last line

The log ends at day 212 with a line of 41 characters, the column reading 212. The array was not recovered, and the wearer's fate is not attested.

## Provenance and ground

Synthesis from the device's log fragments in the Code Deposit, a bench note on filter decay, and two message streams in the Wasatch Corpus; the neuroscience is the period's own, the household identification is not.
