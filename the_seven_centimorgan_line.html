The Seven-Centimorgan Line

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from no single fragment — a synthesis of intercepted verification telemetry in the Wasatch Corpus, eleven printed match cards, and a segment-matching library preserved bit-exact in the Code Deposit — and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

The Seven-Centimorgan Line is the Historical Faculty’s descriptive label for an unnamed Late Combustion automated kinship-verification service, which adjudicated the family-reunification claims of people on the move by measuring the DNA two applicants shared — and whose self-correcting statistical baselines, kept only to keep its arithmetic honest, are now among the few quantitative records of the era’s great redistribution. The scattered scraps of its traffic and the code it ran are record; the service’s name, its operators, and nearly every pair it judged are reconstruction, or gone.

Seven centimorgans — on the genome-wide average, roughly seven million base pairs of matched sequence — was the shortest run of shared DNA the service would count, the floor below which apparent matches are mostly artefact. From about +87 pT (2032 CE), intake stations on the inland and upland corridors submitted applicant pairs to it; the service summed every shared segment above the line and returned a degree of relatedness against the claim made at the desk. It descended, by the Code Deposit’s own commit history, from a consumer pastime of relative-finding, repurposed as a gate. What it was built to decide is largely lost. What it recorded while deciding is not.

Degree, not kind

A child shares one full copy of the map with each parent — about 3,400 centimorgans. Every further degree of kinship halves the expectation, so at one-quarter sharing the mathematics goes blind to kind: a grandmother, a half-brother, and an uncle present the same fraction, and no threshold can tell them apart. The service therefore returned only degree; the operator wrote the kind. One session survives whole in the intercepted telemetry:

RELATIONSHIP CLAIMED: brother. RESULT: 1,893 cM / 41 segments / degree 2. CONSISTENT WITH: half-sibling; grandparent; aunt/uncle. OPERATOR ANNOTATION: passed as brother. same mother, they state. no document either way.

The moving baseline

In populations that had married inward for a hundred generations, the arithmetic faced a second problem: two strangers from the same endogamous group can share dozens of centimorgans in many short segments, enough to counterfeit cousinhood. The service handled this as its designers intended, maintaining a background-sharing baseline for every source group it saw and re-estimating it weekly from the arriving pairs themselves. That correction worked exactly as designed and outlived its purpose: the gate it served lapsed with the authority behind it, but the weekly tables, scattered through the telemetry, kept their own account to the end. Read in series, they fall — corridor by corridor, decade by decade, fastest in the groups arriving from a delta that logged its first wet-bulb excursions in the +120s pT (2060s CE) — background sharing draining out of populations as they mixed on the road. Okonkwo, who classes the tables with the commit timestamps and routing logs as unintended censuses, notes that no instrument of the period was ever commissioned to measure the redistribution; this one measured it weekly, as a by-product of subtraction. Whether the relaxation the tables record was emancipation or catastrophe, the numbers cannot say.

The last dated scrap in the synthesis is a printed match card issued 4 June +129 pT (2074 CE) at an upland intake station: claimed relationship grandmother, computed degree 2, a ration stamp on the reverse.

Provenance and ground

The witness is a single stream — the service’s own traffic — but no single deposit carries it. The weekly baseline tables and one whole session survive in the Wasatch Corpus, with that corpus’s known over-sampling of the connected and the counted; eleven printed match cards survive on laminate; the matching library survives bit-exact in the Code Deposit, every file named by a fingerprint of its own contents and therefore provably original. The service’s name appears in none of them.

Notes

  1. A centimorgan is a unit of genetic map distance, not physical length; the equivalence of one centimorgan to roughly one million base pairs is a genome-wide average and varies severalfold along the chromosomes.
  2. Segments shorter than about seven centimorgans are excluded because short apparent matches arise frequently by chance from the phasing of common variants; the cutoff is the period’s own convention, preserved in the recovered library’s configuration defaults.
  3. All dates are post-Trinity; CE = pT + 1945.
