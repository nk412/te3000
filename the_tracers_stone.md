# The Tracer's Stone

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from an incised granite tablet recovered face-down as a threshold slab, read against forty-one microfilm frames from the upper racks of the Longyear Deposit, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Tracer's Stone is the Historical Faculty's name for a Middle Combustion memorial tablet whose carved text preserves an unfilled substitution field from the machine that composed it, and for the unnamed district disease tracer whose working hand survives on the same office's filmed case cards.** The stone, the film, and the hand are record; whether one person stood behind the office's reasoning, and how many people the tablet was raised over, are reconstruction, and neither has been settled.

---

**The Tracer's Stone is not a grave, no one is buried beneath it, and the sentence cut across its middle was not composed by a person.** It is a memorial tablet cut in +37 pT (1982 CE) for a district notification office in granite upland at 57° N, after an outbreak of enteric fever in week 34 of +36 pT (1981 CE). Its text was produced by the office's weekly report generator, from a standing template with substitution fields; three of the fields were filled by hand on the printer's proof and the fourth was not, so the count of the dead stands in the granite as the variable name itself. A bronze plate carrying the names was bolted below the cut text and is gone. The stone survived a millennium because it was later taken up and laid face-down as a doorstep. Of the officer who ran the office's tracing — who interviewed cases, listed contacts, and kept the district carrier register — nothing survives but a pencil hand on a filmed teaching set of forty-one case cards, and from that set the discipline reconstructed the district's transmission as concentrated almost entirely in a small physiological tail, and credited the office with recognising it. Three readings of that credit are live. The dispute is not over the epidemiology, which is sound; it is over whether there was anyone in the office to hold it.

## The threshold slab

The tablet is one slab of medium-grained biotite granite, 840 by 520 by 90 mm, recovered in 2846 from the widened doorway of a byre at Ardowen. It had been laid face-down, which is why it can be read; its second line dates the outbreak, and the printer's proof discussed below dates the cutting. Weathering rates measured on the period's own exposed gravestones put granite surface recession between five and sixty-five millimetres in a thousand years depending on climate and aspect; the letters here are cut eight millimetres deep, so a millennium of frontal weather in an upland would have taken the inscription off the stone entirely. Halvorsen, E., on whose taphonomy the identification rests, finds the buried face shielded from rainfall, from freeze–thaw cycling in the pore space, and from the lichen thalli that open grain boundaries, and losing surface at a small fraction of the exposed rate. What wear it carries is foot polish, heaviest across the fourth line.

Below the text are four fixing holes of twelve millimetres, their lead plugs still seated and the fixings sheared off flush with the plug faces. Green staining runs a long way down from each hole in the basic copper salts that form as a copper alloy weathers and washes over stone. A plate of that alloy hung there for a long time, and the sheared fixings say it was taken off rather than left to fail. The cut text reads:

```
RAISED BY THE NOTIFICATION OFFICE OF DISTRICT 4
IN THE MATTER OF THE ENTERIC FEVER OF WEEK 34 OF 1981
&D1. DIED IN THIS DISTRICT
THE NAMES ARE BELOW
```

## Forty-one frames

The class of office is well attested, and its standing instrument — the weekly return of notifiable disease, one row per reporting week, one column per disease — is treated at **The Morbidity Return**. This office ran the other half of the work. On a notification of enteric fever it sent an officer to take a history of the case's water, milk, and food handling, to list household and occupational contacts, and to obtain clearance specimens afterward: not one negative specimen but a run of them, because a person who has cleared the organism and a person who is shedding it intermittently produce the same single negative. Those who failed to clear went onto the district carrier register and were excluded from food handling, in some jurisdictions for life.

What survives of that work is forty-one frames of silver-halide microfilm, made at +44 pT (1989 CE) for a regional training course and consigned at +72 pT (2017 CE) into the Longyear Deposit, where they went onto an upper rack and so were above the acidified water when the chamber flooded and dissolved the film below. Thirty-one frames are case cards annotated in pencil in a single hand, palaeographically consistent across dates from +30 pT (1975 CE) to +39 pT (1984 CE): secondary cases attributed, contacts counted, running totals in the margin. On nine cards the rate column carries the same note, *denom. unk.* Frames 39 to 41 are not case cards. They were catalogued as administrative and not read until 2793. Frame 40 is the office's standing copy sheet for the weekly bulletin. Frame 39 is the printer's proof of the memorial text, with three fields filled in ink. The fourth was the deaths.

## Where the tail lives

The quantity the cards support is the offspring distribution: for each traced case, the number of further cases attributed to it. Its mean is the reproduction number, and its shape is the whole of the epidemiology. Where that distribution is Poisson, cases are interchangeable and control can be aimed at the average. Where it is negative binomial with a small dispersion parameter, they are not: most cases infect no one, and a few infect very many. The period established this on its own late material — for one emergent respiratory pathogen of +58 pT (2003 CE) the dispersion parameter was fitted at 0.16, against the Poisson limit of infinity, with seventy-three per cent of cases barely transmitting and about six per cent responsible for more than eight onward infections each. The general rule of thumb the period drew from such fits, that roughly a fifth of cases carry four-fifths of transmission, is the same fact stated coarsely.

For enteric fever the tail is not a matter of how many people a case meets. It is anatomical. Onset follows exposure commonly at a week to a fortnight; the acute case is ill, is in bed, and is a poor transmitter. But three to five per cent of untreated cases fail to clear the organism within a year, and most of those are carrying it in the gallbladder, where it persists as biofilm on the surface of gallstones and is shed into the gut intermittently, in some people for decades. Challenge data from the period fix the dose required: one to ten thousand organisms, given to unvaccinated adults, produced illness in fifty-five to sixty-five per cent of them. A carrier in a kitchen clears that threshold without effort. One urban estimate from the period's own literature puts the mean reproduction number near three where the water carries it; in a district with a clean spring supply the water term is small and nearly all the mean sits in a handful of people who feel perfectly well.

That shape has two consequences the cards are annotated around. Greater heterogeneity at the same mean makes most introductions die out by chance in the first generations, and makes the ones that do not die out larger; and control aimed at the tail is far more efficient than control aimed at the average. It also means the instrument built to find the tail is the instrument worst suited to it: intermittent shedding is exactly what a single clearance specimen cannot detect, so the carriers are systematically under-ascertained in proportion to how quietly they carry.

## Selected to teach

Every estimate of the district's dispersion published before 2793 was computed from the forty-one frames as though they were the office's caseload, and several regional syntheses of Middle Combustion enteric fever took their heterogeneity term from that work. The forty-one frames are a teaching set. They were chosen by the office, from an index of unknown size, for being worth showing to trainees.

Ranganathan, on whose treatment of sampling and bias the correction rests, sets out the two selection criteria and shows that they pull opposite ways. A chain is teachable if it is complete — if every contact was followed and every secondary case accounted for — and completeness is easiest to achieve in small chains, which truncates the tail and makes transmission look more even than it was. A chain is also teachable if it is instructive, and the instructive chain is the large branching one, which oversamples clusters in proportion to their size and makes transmission look more concentrated than it was. The net of the two cannot be recovered, because recovering it would require the sampling frame, and the sampling frame is the rest of the index, which does not exist. The set is admissible as evidence of what the office understood and inadmissible as a sample of what the district suffered. The disciplinary index accordingly carries the district's fitted dispersion as indicative only, and no regional synthesis may cite it.

## Whether anyone was in the office

Frame 40 settled nothing and reopened everything. The standing copy sheet is a template in the macro language of a statistical package, run weekly against the tabulated returns on an installation the district shared with the regional authority:

```
&HEAD. — WEEK &WK. OF &YR.
&N1. CASES NOTIFIED. &D1. DIED IN THIS DISTRICT.
%IF &CR. > &CR0. %THEN %STR(THE WORK OF THIS OFFICE IS WITH THE
  CARRIERS AND NOT WITH THE WELLS.);
```

The final sentence had been quoted for two centuries as the tracer's own formulation of the tail argument. It is a conditional branch, emitted whenever a ratio held. The same sentence, in the same wording, appears on a second district's filmed bulletin sheet elsewhere in the consignment.

Three readings are argued. On the **hand reading**, the template is the tracer's argument made permanent: a person who writes *denom. unk.* across a rate column is a person with a position on what the numbers mean, the hand and the copy sheet are contemporaneous, and an office that had not understood the tail would not have written the condition that fires on it. On the **post reading**, the hand is a hand doing arithmetic and nothing more; the analysis belongs to the post rather than to its holder, the second district's identical sentence being a circular from a tier above, and the phrase reaching the stone by the same route it reached the bulletin. On the **generator reading**, associated with Marchetti, who had already dismantled the reading of a trivial period routine as a canonical text, the question is not which person composed the sentence but whether the record can carry the answer: prose emitted under a rule cannot be assigned to a mind, and the office's surviving voice is entirely emitted prose.

The dispute cannot be closed from the material. Everything in the office's hand is arithmetic, and everything that argues is output. No later scholarship has produced a sentence from this district that is both analytical and demonstrably written by someone, and the convention of citation — *the District 4 tracer* — names an attested hand with no established holder.

## Denominator unknown

A count of cases is a numerator. To make it a rate the office needed the population at risk, and the district's register was compiled at intervals of years in a place where quarry and forestry labour arrived and left inside a reporting week. Frame 27 carries an attempt at the standard remedy: the two-list estimator set out in +4 pT (1949 CE) by two statisticians working on the completeness of birth and death registration, in which a population is estimated from two incomplete lists of it as the product of their sizes divided by their overlap. The annotation gives two list totals, a matched count, and a struck-out product. The method assumes the population is closed over the period and that appearing on one list does not alter the chance of appearing on the other. Neither held: people moved through, and a physician who bothered to notify was the physician likelier to send the specimen, so the two lists overlapped for reasons that had nothing to do with the disease and the estimate came out low. The figure is struck through and the column is annotated in the usual three letters.

What the office could count, it counted well. What it could not count was who was there, and the shortfall is not a failure of care but a property of the instrument: a notification system measures events and has no organ for measuring the people events happen to. The district's cases are known to the case; its population is known to nothing. The lead plugs remain in all four holes, and the plate they anchored is gone, and the count it was raised over stands in the granite as a field the office never filled.

## Provenance and ground

Two witnesses, physically independent and joined by one document. The tablet is record: its stone, its cut depth, its fixing holes, and its copper staining are measured, and its second line dates the event it was raised for. The film is record: forty-one frames, provenance-clean, on an upper rack of the Longyear Deposit and therefore above the pyritic water that dissolved the lower racks of the same chamber. The join is frame 39, the printer's proof, whose unfilled fourth field is the field cut into the stone. Everything else is reconstruction: the district's boundaries, the size of the index from which the teaching set was drawn, the identity and number of the holders of the hand, and the count the bronze carried. The tablet cannot be radiocarbon dated, and neither can anything else of its age.

## Notes

Dating is post-Trinity: CE = pT + 1945. The identification of the fever as enteric rests on the clearance-specimen columns and the carrier register implied by the cards, not on any clinical description, none of which survives. The attribution of the pencil annotations to a single writer is palaeographic and is not disputed; what a single writer implies about a single officer is the matter of the entry. The dispersion figures quoted from the period's own late literature are that literature's, measured on its own outbreaks; the district's fitted value is carried as indicative and is not cited here.
