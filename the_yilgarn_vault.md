# The Yilgarn Vault

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from an engraved instruction plate, a partial disc rack recovered from a sealed side gallery of the adit, and a distributed contract ledger cross-held across four unrelated custodial sites, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Yilgarn Vault is the Historical Faculty's name for a Consolidation-era data-perpetuity facility cut into one of the oldest stable granite bodies on the planet, which sold both institutional archiving under a mathematically guaranteed redundancy and a cheaper personal-message service that carried none.** The optical medium, the vault's structure, and a fraction of its contents are physical record, read out voxel by voxel four centuries after the operator stopped answering; which of the personal messages were composed by the people who paid for them, and which were never written by anyone at all, is not recoverable, and the record itself is the reason why.

---

**In +403 pT (2348 CE), two centuries into the recovery that followed the Contraction, a chamber cut forty metres into granite dated to the mid-Archaean was sealed, pressurised with dry nitrogen, and put into commercial service as a repository rated to outlast the institutions that built it by a factor its own engraved plate gave as nine orders of magnitude.** The medium was optical: fused silica disc blanks, each written by a tightly focused pulsed laser into a lattice of self-organised nanoscale voids, and read back by measuring how each void bends polarised light. The physics itself is old — the writing method dates from well before the Contraction, a Combustion Age laboratory technique carried forward through the recovery on the same handful of surviving optics texts every laser shop of the Consolidation learns from — but the vault, its tiers of service, and its clientele are the Historical Faculty's reconstruction from what one gallery of it left behind. It held two kinds of promise to two kinds of customer, and kept only one of them.

## Voxels in stone

Each disc blank is plain vitreous silica, chemically and structurally the most inert glass the age made. A femtosecond laser, focused a few micrometres beneath the surface, deposits enough energy in a volume smaller than a bacterium to rearrange the glass network locally into a birefringent nanograting — a self-assembled stack of nanoscale voids, aligned by the polarisation of the writing pulse, that retards one axis of light passing through it more than the perpendicular axis. A voxel's position fixes three coordinates (two lateral, one by depth of focus, allowing scores of written layers through a few millimetres of glass); the strength of its retardance and the azimuth of its slow axis, both set independently by the writing pulse and both read out independently by a polarisation microscope afterward, supply two more. Five addressable quantities per point in a solid block of glass is the origin of the trade's own name for the technique, coined without apparent embarrassment: **five-dimensional recording**. The vault's format bank quantised retardance and azimuth to four bits between them, giving each voxel roughly the information capacity of a hexadecimal digit; a disc blank the size and thickness of a coaster carried, in the vault's own advertised figure, upward of 360 terabytes.

The durability claim was not invented for the brochure. Silica bonds broken and re-formed by the writing pulse do not anneal at ambient temperature on any timescale a customer could object to; accelerated-ageing tests of the era held written blanks at temperatures up to 1,000 °C and extrapolated, by the same Arrhenius reasoning used to age-rate the electrical insulation of the period's own transformers, a room-temperature data lifetime measured in the tens of billions of years — longer than the medium's own manufacturing process, longer than the star that will eventually vaporise it. Nothing in the four centuries between the vault's sealing and its reopening has disturbed a single written voxel. The glass did exactly what it was sold to do. The vault's failure, where it failed, was never the glass.

## Two tiers of redundancy

The vault sold storage two ways, and the difference between them is arithmetic rather than commercial colour. A payload is first split into *k* equal-sized data shards; a systematic Reed–Solomon code, worked over a finite field in the standard way of the period's error-correcting mathematics, then computes *m* additional parity shards such that **any** *k* of the resulting *k + m* shards — in whatever combination, data or parity — are sufficient to reconstruct the whole. This is the defining property of a maximum-distance-separable code: redundancy purchased not as a copy of the data but as an algebraic surplus over it, so that the loss of any *m* shards, not merely a foreseen *m*, is survivable. The vault's institutional tier wrote archives as sixteen shards, twelve of data and four of parity, and — this was the paid-for part — distributed the sixteen across four physically separate custodial sites under contract, so that the loss of an entire site, granite adit and all, still left the archive whole. Contracts obliged each custodian to re-verify and, on any detected fault, re-derive and rewrite its shard on a fixed cycle; the schedule and the four custodians' names are given, redundantly, on the vault's own engraved leader plate and in an independent registry the Faculty located through an unrelated deposit, so this part of the record is secure by the same logic it describes.

The personal-message tier, sold under the trade's own name for it — *the legacy write* — took one payload, split it into no shards, computed no parity, and etched it once, onto one disc, in one vault. It was priced accordingly, at a fraction of the institutional rate, and marketed on the medium's own multi-billion-year figure: correctly, as far as the glass was concerned. What the marketing did not distinguish, and perhaps did not itself track as a separate risk, was that the vault's directory of *which disc held whose message* — the mapping from a customer's order number to a physical shelf position and a byte offset — was not written to any disc at all. It lived, as such things ordinarily did, in the operator's own transactional database, on infrastructure rented and renewed like any other business record of the period. The scholar Ibarra, N., in the standing doctrine of single-filament infrastructure, would recognise the shape immediately: an archive rated to outlast the Solar System's own comfortable stellar lifetime, made to depend, for its legibility, on one un-mirrored database with an ordinary subscription. The cost of giving that index the same four-site redundancy given to the exabytes it pointed to was, by the operator's own surviving cost model, judged not worth passing on to a customer buying a keepsake rather than a national archive. The subscription lapsed a few decades on, for reasons the surviving trace does not give, in the same interval-mismatch failure that had already cost the previous age most of its own digital record, recurring here in miniature and on a single company's books. The glass did not need a subscription.

## The unclaimed rack

The gallery the Faculty recovered was not the institutional archive — that survives, whole and legible, at whichever of the four original custodial sites a given scholar consults, exactly as designed. It was a side chamber the vault's own contract called, in the one surviving fragment of tariff schedule, **long hold**: personal-tier discs whose named recipient never submitted the claim procedure the service required to release a copy, for reasons the vault's paperwork does not give and the Faculty does not supply. Long-hold discs were retained on-site indefinitely at no further charge, an obligation the operator appears to have honoured to the end of its own operating life; the last serviced entry on the vault's maintenance log is dated +441 pT (2386 CE), close to four centuries before the Institute's resurvey found the chamber again, and nothing suggests it was touched by anyone who understood what it held in the meantime.

The rack itself needed no protection the granite did not already provide; the Yilgarn craton is among the most seismically and hydrologically stable bodies of continental crust on the planet, chosen for exactly that reason, and the recovered discs show no measurable fault the Faculty's polarisation scanners can attribute to time rather than to the writing process itself. The chamber was found, by the Institute for Deep Record Studies survey that reopened it, with 2,206 discs racked in their original order and a numbered brass tag on each shelf position — a tag that once matched an entry in the vanished database and now matches nothing. The discs are perfect. The catalogue that would have named their senders and their intended readers is gone as completely as if it had never been written, because in the sense that matters it very nearly wasn't: one copy, unmirrored, on a machine no one thought worth the additional contract line.

## Reading the blind shelf

Recovering content from an unindexed disc is not decryption; nothing on the long-hold rack was ever enciphered. It is closer to the problem an epigrapher faces with an inscription in a known script but an unknown text boundary — the bits are legible the instant they are read out, and the difficulty is entirely one of segmentation. The Faculty's approach follows the vault's own institutional-tier practice, which is documented, as a control: read the raw voxel stream in the fixed order the leader plate specifies, and look for a container format identical to the one the institutional shards use, since both tiers were written by the same facility on the same equipment and there is no evidence the operator maintained two encoding pipelines.

The institutional archive's container turned out to be traceable to a specific open-source compression library, identified by matching its file-header signature and internal block structure against source held complete and content-verified in the Code Deposit — one of the few instances in the discipline where a recovered format can be checked, byte for byte, against the original program that wrote it, rather than inferred from the bitstream alone. Applying the same container assumption to the long-hold voxel stream lets the Faculty find file boundaries by a cruder but real method: Shannon entropy, estimated over a sliding window of the readout, falls sharply at a header and rises to near the theoretical maximum across compressed payload, so a boundary between two personal messages shows up as a brief trough in an otherwise flat, near-random signal. Of the 2,206 discs, 1,140 yield at least one cleanly segmented, fully decompressible message this way. The remainder either failed to decompress — a single flipped bit in an entropy-coded stream corrupts everything downstream of it, and with no parity shard to correct against, the institutional tier's Reed–Solomon safety net simply was not there to catch it — or contain a boundary the entropy method cannot resolve with confidence, and are read as *present, unsegmented*, a status the Faculty's disciplinary index reserves for exactly this kind of failure.

## What came back

The leader plate at the adit mouth states its own terms in full, in the standardised legal language the era used for such notices: *THIS ARCHIVE IS WRITTEN TO A PHYSICAL MEDIUM RATED FOR CONTINUOUS LEGIBILITY UNDER NORMAL STORAGE CONDITIONS IN EXCESS OF TEN THOUSAND YEARS. NO WARRANTY IS MADE AS TO THE CONTINUITY OF ANY ASSOCIATED SERVICE, INDEX, OR CUSTODIAL ARRANGEMENT BEYOND THE TERM OF THE ORIGINAL CONTRACT.* The clause is, on its own terms, honest; it is also the entire explanation, stated flatly and in advance, for why 1,066 of the 2,206 messages cannot be read and why none of the 1,140 that can be read carries the name of the person who wrote it.

Among the legible messages, a recurring register appears across discs the Faculty can otherwise show shared no purchaser, no address, and no plausible connection: near-identical phrasing, clustered heavily around a small number of sentence templates, differing mainly in the proper names dropped into them. One such fragment, recovered whole, reads: *"...we are so proud of the person you have becom— "* and stops mid-word at a read error the entropy method flags but cannot repair. Whether this register reflects the vault's own order interface — which the surviving tariff fragment confirms offered a pre-filled default message to any customer who reached checkout without having composed one — or simply the ordinary convergence of unrelated people reaching, under time pressure, for the same handful of things one says to someone left behind, the Faculty cannot determine disc by disc. The scholar Marchetti, L., who elsewhere in the discipline dismantled a reading of an incidental software routine as a deliberately authored text, treats this as the harder and unresolved case in the other direction: not machine text mistaken for human, but human intent and machine default converged so completely in surface form that no internal feature of the message distinguishes them, and no external record — the one record that could have — survived to settle it.

Two readings sit over the long-hold rack without resolving each other. One holds that the convergence is the finding: that a service sold as a vessel for something irreplaceably personal was, for a measurable fraction of its customers, quietly filled in on their behalf, and the durability of the medium preserved a default rather than a person. The other holds that the words were never the point of a message written to be read across a gulf the sender could not picture and would not survive to see acknowledged — that choosing to send something, and paying for it to outlast you, is the act the record shows, whatever furnished the sentence. Nothing in the 1,140 recovered messages, and nothing in the 1,066 that remain unsegmented noise, decides between the two.

## Provenance and ground

The institutional archive is cross-held, per its original distributed contract, at all four of its named custodial sites and is not seriously contested. The long-hold rack rests on the Institute for Deep Record Studies' +830 pT (2775 CE) resurvey of the Yilgarn adit, its 2,206-disc inventory, and the polarisation-scan readout of each; the segmentation method is standard entropy-based format recovery, checked against the institutional container identified in the Code Deposit. The single-point failure of the vault's customer index is Ibarra, N.'s reading, in the standing doctrine of single-filament infrastructure; the contested authorship of the templated messages is Marchetti, L.'s. No maker's mark, corporate seal, or trading name survives on any disc, plate, or fragment of contract; contemporary disclosure convention required the leader plate to state terms, not a brand, and the Faculty designates the facility by its locality alone.
