The Ghaggar Sweat Line

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a run of fired-ceramic telemetry tablets in a salt working and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

A heat-tolerant dairy line of the Late Combustion Age, known only from a run of 827 ceramic tablets that logged its body temperatures hourly for 827 days, 414 of them after there was no living animal left to log. The tablets and the temperatures are record; the cause of the line’s end is reconstruction, and three readings of it stand.

Fifty-one crates of fired tablets, each about the size of a hand and glazed on one face, were taken from a side gallery of the salt working that holds the Hallstatt tablets. Each carries a legend, SWEAT LINE 7 / GHAGGAR, and twenty-four rows of figures. The legend is the only place the line’s name survives. The line was a herd of high-yield dairy cattle carrying a dense-sweat-gland, short-coat inheritance, bred from +98 pT (2043 CE) for work on the Indo-Gangetic plain and logged through rumen boluses reporting to one barn controller. The tablets record a rumen temperature climbing past 41 °C in the barn’s last thirty-one hours, then a smooth decline. They go on recording for 414 days.

The limit

A high-yield cow of some 600 kg sheds on the order of a kilowatt of metabolic heat. Sweat carries it away at about 2.4 megajoules per kilogram evaporated, so each kilowatt wants roughly 1.5 litres of water an hour, and the line was bred to supply it. Breeding could widen the supply. It could not move the ceiling: an evaporating surface cannot be cooled below the wet-bulb temperature of the air passing over it. A skin that must be held near 35 °C, in air at a wet-bulb of 35 °C, sheds nothing by sweat, whatever the density of its glands. The barn’s chilled-water loop existed to hold the air under that figure.

The readings

The plant reading (Ibarra) holds that the loop failed and the line was not the limit: a herd bred for 34 °C wet-bulb would have outlasted a dry pump. The limit reading holds that the loop ran, that the barn’s intake air touched a wet-bulb of 35.2 °C in the +124 pT (2069 CE) excursion, and that no pump in the herd’s history would have saved it. The triage reading (Zhou-Ferreira) holds that the operator culled the herd to spare it, and that the decline is a body cooling after an act, not after a failure.

The tablets favour none. A bolus reads only while it lies in a body, and a carcass of this mass in still air cools toward ambient with a time constant of order a day, whatever stopped the heart. The pump field cannot arbitrate: it logs the commanded state, and the command read 1 on every row.

From day 623 the bolus and the air agree to a tenth of a degree.

End of record

The intake machine fired one tablet per feed-day from a prepaid standing order and stopped on day 827, when its glaze stock ran out. The last tablet carries twenty-four rows of identical figures, and the controller’s setpoint line, HELD.

Provenance and ground

The tablets were recovered from the Hallstatt working in a side gallery lodged by a dairy cooperative; the salt kept them dry, and the vitrified body, fired near 1,250 °C, kept the legend. The consignment shows the bias of every deposit-lodged source: it records the herd of a connected cooperative, and the herds of those without one are not in it.
