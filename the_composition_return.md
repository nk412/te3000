# The Composition Return

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a corrosion-resistant nickel plate carrying the reference framework and a run of specimen returns of a Late Combustion consumer ancestry service, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Composition Return is the Historical Faculty's name for the archive of a subscription service that, for a fee and a swab of saliva, returned to each customer a set of percentages naming the reference populations their genome most resembled — an archive the discipline read for two generations as a direct census of who lived where before recognising that it measured distance from a panel of samples and not ancestry at all.** The plate, its framework, and its specimen returns are bit-exact record; that the percentages describe the age's population structure, as the early reconstructions took them to, is the error the reading has since retracted.

---

**The Composition Return is not a census, not a map of ancestry, and not a record of where anyone came from.** It is the surviving archive of a commercial service of one Anglophone market which, across the Late Combustion Age and into the Contraction, sold a person a reading of their own genome expressed as a small table of fractions — *so many parts in a hundred* of this reference population, so many of that — computed by comparing that genome against a stored panel of other people's. What physically survives is one nickel plate of the corrosion-resistant consignment, struck at +147 pT (2092 CE), carrying the service's reference framework and roughly forty specimen returns issued between +133 and +161 pT (2078 and 2106 CE). The early discipline read the plate as a population record: it took the reference populations for real ancestral groups and the fractions for a measured distribution, and built on them an account of who the age's people were and where they stood. The account did not survive contact with the genetics. The fractions are not ancestry but resemblance to a panel; the panel over-sampled the few who bought the service; the reference populations against which every fraction was reckoned are, by the service's own science, no more ancestral or unmixed than the customers measured against them. The method resolved a paying customer to a single district and could not place, even to a continent, the populations it measured everyone else against. In the current deposit catalogue the plate is listed not under population records but under recovered product literature.

## The swab and the panel

The service's mechanism is recoverable in full from the framework struck on the plate, and it is ordinary period genomics rather than anything invented for the trade. A customer's saliva yielded cells; the cells yielded DNA; the DNA was read not base by base but at a fixed grid of informative sites — between half a million and seven hundred thousand single-nucleotide positions where members of the species are known to differ — genotyped on a manufactured array. The resulting string of variants was then phased into its two inherited copies and cut into windows along each chromosome, and each window was assigned to whichever *reference population* in the stored panel its variants most resembled, a preliminary window-by-window call afterward smoothed along the chromosome by a hidden Markov model that corrected the isolated misassignments a noisy window throws off. The genome came out the far side as a mosaic — this stretch resembling one panel population, that stretch another — and the fractions the customer received were simply the mosaic totalled: the share of the genome's length assigned to each reference population, rendered to the nearest percent and drawn as a divided circle.

Everything in that chain is a real and checkable operation. What the customer was not sold, and what the plate's framework states plainly in its own technical leader for anyone who read past the circle, is what the fractions are fractions *of*. They are not proportions of descent. They are proportions of resemblance to a particular stored panel of particular sampled people, and they mean only as much as that panel does.

## What the panel was made of

A reference population, in the framework's own definition, was a set of contributed genomes whose donors reported that all four of their grandparents had been born in one named place. This is a construction, not a found fact, and it was the load-bearing construction of the whole service. It supposed that four grandparents from one place fixed a genome to that place; it supposed the named places were separable; and it supposed, above all, that such a set could stand as the *unmixed* origin from which a customer's admixture was measured. The period's own population genetics denied the last supposition outright. No human population is unmixed. Every one is itself the mosaic of older movements, so that a panel of the pure is a panel of a thing that does not occur, and the service's reference populations were admixtures pressed into service as origins because a coordinate frame needs an origin and the demand was for one.

The panel was biased in a second way, mechanically and traceably. The service grew where genomes were cheap to gather and customers able to pay, and both conditions held densest in one connected demographic — so that of the reference genomes the panel drew on, on the order of seventy-eight in every hundred came from a population that was itself about a sixth of the living species. The consequence is not subtle. Because a window can only be assigned to a population the panel contains, and can be resolved finely only where the panel is dense, the service returned to a well-sampled customer an ancestry cut to the district — a valley, a single county — and lumped the whole of the thinly-sampled remainder of humanity into a handful of coarse continental blocks. The resolution of the answer tracked the wealth and connectedness of the sampled and not the diversity of the world. It ran, in fact, exactly against that diversity: genetic variation within the species is greatest nearest the source of its long expansion and thins with distance from it, in a decline close to linear with the road travelled, so that the most various populations on Earth were the ones the panel held most thinly and resolved most crudely, and the least various — the ones farthest down the road of the expansion — were the ones it knew in finest grain. The service saw humanity at a resolution inverse to humanity's actual variety.

## The scale of what the fractions never were

The figure the fractions most obscured is the one the period had established a century before the service existed and never overturned. Of all the genetic variation in the species, about six parts in seven lie *within* any single population; well under a tenth distinguishes the broadest continental groupings from one another, and by the densest single-nucleotide counts the between-group share falls to a few percent, a fixation index of order a tenth or less. Two people drawn from one town differ, at the great majority of variable sites, by nearly as much as two people drawn from opposite ends of the Earth. The divided circle inverted the proportion it was drawn from: it rendered the small between-group fraction as the whole of a person, gave it colours and borders, and left unshown the overwhelming shared remainder that the same data held. The circle was not false in its arithmetic. Every fraction on the plate recomputes correctly from the framework. It was false in what it let the fraction stand for.

## Read as a census

The plate reached the Faculty as one of the cleanest genomic survivals of the Contraction — bit-exact, self-describing, with a legible technical leader — and it was read, for two generations, as what it appeared to be: a register of the age's population structure, tabulated by a contemporary who had measured it. The specimen returns were treated as a sample of real people; their fractions were treated as those people's ancestry; and the reference populations, being named and bounded on the plate, were treated as the ancestral groups of the age. On that reading the discipline assembled a demography — proportions of this origin and that in the Contraction-era population, a map of where the age's peoples stood and in what mixture — and cited it, because so little else of the period speaks to population at all, and here was a source that appeared to speak to nothing else. The reading was careful, quantitative, and wrong at its foundation, and its care is exactly what made it durable. A source that gives numbers to a discipline starved of them is believed in proportion to the numbers, and this one gave them to the percent.

The error is the one Marchetti isolates as the discipline's standing hazard with recovered artefacts: mistaking a thing made *for* the age's people for a measurement *of* them. The returns were a product. Their unit of analysis was a paying subscriber, not a sampled inhabitant; their geography was the geography of the panel, not of the world; and their numbers described the panel's shape as much as any customer's. To read them as a census was to reconstruct a population from the resemblances of its wealthiest and most connected members to a reference set assembled out of those same members — a circle closed so neatly that its output looked like independent confirmation of its input. Ranganathan's caution against the intercepted advertising corpus applies without change: the source tells us not what the age's people were, but what was computed about the ones who bought a computation.

## The correction

The correction, when it came, was made in the genetics and not in the archive, and it was made dryly. Bekele's reworking of the plate (Bekele, H., *Historiography*, on the composition returns as reference artefacts) does not dispute a single fraction. It re-derives them, confirms the arithmetic, and then observes what the arithmetic rests on. A fraction is a distance from the panel; the panel is a set of admixed populations declared unmixed; the declaration is one the same science forbids; therefore the fractions are internally exact statements about an origin that does not exist, and they measure a real quantity — resemblance to specific stored samples — that is not the quantity the reading had taken them for. None of this required new evidence. It required only reading the framework's own leader, which had said as much, in the technical register the divided circle was designed to spare the customer.

The turn Bekele lands, and leaves, is the one the whole archive is built around. The service could resolve a well-sampled customer's ancestry to a single district and could return no ancestry at all for its own reference populations, because a coordinate cannot be given its own coordinates; and the populations it could place least well of all — that it lumped into the crudest blocks or could not distinguish from the reference itself — were the connected, cosmopolitan, much-admixed people who had built and bought the service, whose genomes were the mixture the method existed to decompose and were, for that reason, the mixture it could least decompose. The instrument that placed everyone failed on the population that made it. It is stated in Bekele once, mid-paragraph, and not returned to.

## What is still cited

The reading is retracted but not everywhere disused, and the discipline does not pretend otherwise. In the regions and generations where the Contraction destroyed every civil record, the composition returns are among the only period sources that carry any population signal at all, and a strand of demographic reconstruction still draws on them — not as a census now, but as a bounded proxy, flagged provisional, its fractions read for the panel-relative resemblances they honestly are and not for the ancestry they were sold as. Whether even that residual use is sound is not settled. One school holds that a resemblance to a known panel, honestly bounded, is real information about a real genome and may be used as such where nothing else survives, the panel's bias being correctable once named. The other holds that a source whose every value is contaminated by the shape of its own reference set cannot be decontaminated after the fact, because the correction would need the very distribution the source was mistaken for; that a proxy built on a closed circle stays closed however carefully it is flagged; and that the honest course is to carry the plate as product literature and read no population from it at all. The two schools rest on the same secure facts — the framework, the panel, the fractions, the bias — and part on whether a known bias is a removable one. The Faculty adjudicates neither. The demographic account assembled on the first reading is carried in the standing bibliography with its own entry struck through and the single word *superseded*, and the plate's specimen returns are now cited, where cited at all, as the service's claims and not as the Faculty's findings.

## Provenance and ground

The entry rests on one nickel plate of the corrosion-resistant consignment, struck at +147 pT (2092 CE) and recovered intact, its engraved surface legible in full: a technical leader describing the array, the phasing, the window classification, and the panel; a schematic of the reference framework naming its populations and their donor criterion; and roughly forty specimen returns, each a subscriber identifier, a genotype summary, and a table of panel-relative fractions, dated across +133 to +161 pT (2078 and 2106 CE). Halvorsen's survey of the consignment accounts for the plate's survival by its medium and for the loss of the service's bulk records — held on leased custodial storage — by the ordinary lapse of that storage when its subscription lapsed; the plate survived because it was metal and the database did not because it was rented. The plate was struck, on the evidence of its own dedicatory line, as a legacy deposit, a durable self-portrait of a going concern, in the manner of the age's deliberate archives. It carries, accordingly, the service at its most confident and least qualified, which is the condition in which it was first read. The population genetics against which it is now read — the apportionment of variation within and between groups, the fixation index, the decline of diversity with distance from the expansion's source, the admixed character of all populations — is the period's own established science and not this entry's. What the specimen subscribers' ancestry actually was is not on the plate and is not reconstructed here.

## Notes

- The chain from swab to fraction — the informative-site array, the phasing, the windowed classification against a reference panel, the hidden-Markov smoothing, and the mosaic totalled to a divided circle — is the period's standard ancestry-deconvolution pipeline. The donor criterion of four grandparents from one place, the resolution's dependence on panel density, and the attenuation of confidence with distance from the reference are the method's own documented properties, not this entry's.
- The apportionment of human genetic variation — the large majority within any population, the small remainder between the broadest groupings — and the near-linear decline of heterozygosity with distance from the source of the species' expansion are established results of the period's population genetics. The fractions on the plate recompute correctly from the framework; the correction concerns not their arithmetic but the reference they are reckoned against.
