# The Channel Commit Log

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a content-addressed commit history in the Code Deposit and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Channel Commit Log is the Historical Faculty's name for 2,114 fingerprinted commits to a turbulence solver whose lost name is recovered as "the unrippled," held in consignment L-III of the Longyear Deposit.** The commits are record; what the solver was for is reconstruction.

---

**The Channel Commit Log** shows one test only: a flow between flat walls at a friction Reynolds number of 180, its box sized to hold the near-wall streaks, which recur about a hundred wall units apart. A school of 2470 CE (+525 pT) read this as the solver's whole domain. Direct simulation, however, needs grid points rising roughly as the 9/4 power of Reynolds number and cost as the cube, so a workshop could afford 180 and little above. The log is the part the authors could pay to verify, mistaken for a sample of what they ran. The last human commit falls in 2074 CE (+129 pT); 611 automated nightly commits follow, each re-running the 180 case and passing, the last in 2075 CE (+130 pT). Nothing was ever recorded of the flow being solved.

## Provenance and ground

Every commit is verifiable by its fingerprint. Okonkwo, D. reads the nightly run as an unintended clock.
