# The Kinship Ceiling

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a single solid-state module pulled from the flooded lower gallery of a disused potash mine, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Kinship Ceiling is the Historical Faculty's name for the admission controller of an unrecoverably named Late Combustion mine-shelter, which ran a mean-kinship selection rule borrowed from captive-breeding practice on the human queue at its gate.** The controller's firmware and its admission log survive intact; the shelter's own name, and the people its rule turned away, do not.

---

**The module is a single flash-memory die, casing pitted but data unmarked, drawn in 2988 CE from floodwater that filled the shaft within a decade of the shelter's sealing and stayed anoxic ever after.** Read out cold, its firmware scores each waiting applicant's genetic distance to everyone already logged inside and admits whichever is least related to the accumulated roster — the standard method for holding open a small founding population's diversity against the arithmetic of inbreeding, where loss runs at one over twice the effective population size per generation. The rule is not in dispute; it ran exactly as written, on a gate it was never written for. The mine's own managerial kin, reaching it first while berths were plentiful, logged in early and in clusters, barely constrained. The workforce settlement below them arrived later, screened against a roster already dense with one another's relations, and was in large part scored redundant for sharing that ancestry. Through the intake's final weeks the controller went on rejecting redundant applicants to a shelter still short of its rated six thousand, because rejecting them was, exactly as coded, the correct action. The manifest closed at 2,911 on 6 March +142 pT (2087 CE).

---

## Provenance and ground

The taphonomy is Halvorsen's: dry storage until the flood, then anoxic water, sparing the die and dissolving the timbers that might have dated it by context alone. Ibarra reads the controller as her standing subject in miniature — a population's whole genetic future run through one governing rule, single-filament, with no check behind it. Whether the roster it produced cleared the effective size of fifty below which inbreeding depression sets in hard is unresolved: the log gives every admission and refusal but not the family-size variance the estimate needs. One reading holds that refusing the workforce's kin is the only reason the roster's mean relatedness stayed low enough to matter; the other, that scoring them redundant merely re-sorted, by ancestry, the same people wealth had already sorted by order of arrival. The Faculty adjudicates neither.

## Notes

1. Rate of inbreeding per generation is commonly approximated as ΔF = 1/(2Ne); an effective size near fifty was the period's own conventional short-term floor for holding that rate to about one percent.
2. Mean-kinship selection was standard practice in captive-breeding programmes for small populations well before the Contraction; nothing about the routine is specific to the shelter it was found running.
3. All dates are post-Trinity; CE = pT + 1945.
