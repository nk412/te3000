The Provisioned Bee

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a diverted rail consignment of leaf-lined nest cells and the genome read from their dormant occupants, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

A mass-provisioned pollinator of the Late Combustion seed-farms, known from a single waterlogged consignment of leaf-lined nest cells, whose poleward march the discipline first read as a wild species tracking the warming and later as the shipping manifest of a retreating agriculture. The cells and the genome are record, and the range they trace is secure; what that range means — a climate signal or a provisioning record — is reconstruction, and the reading has been reversed once already.

In +112 pT (2057 CE) a rail consignment of overwintering pollinator cells was diverted, waterlogged, and never delivered; recovered as an assemblage of leaf-lined nest cells and a genome read from their dormant occupants, it is the type record of a managed solitary bee whose poleward advance the discipline first mistook for a wild species tracking the warming. The bee was a mass-provisioned pollinator of the Late Combustion seed-farms — one bottlenecked stock, incubated on a schedule, trucked to bloom and cleaned back into cold storage, incapable of a self-sustaining wild population without human provisioning. Its cells appear in progressively higher and more northerly sediments across half a century, a clean biotic signal that was read, for a generation of scholarship, as the biosphere keeping pace with the heat. It was not. It was a map of where the farms had gone.

The stock

The lineage is not native to the ground that holds it. It was carried in — an accidental introduction of the Middle Combustion — then domesticated into the most intensively managed solitary bee of the age. Unlike a hive insect it kept no colony: each female cut ovals from leaves, rolled them into thimble cells, mass-provisioned every cell with a loaf of pollen and a single egg, and sealed it. The cells were the product. They were sold loose by volume — of order ten thousand to the gallon — cleaned, graded, overwintered as diapausing prepupae, then incubated in spring to synchronise emergence with bloom. One provisioning note recovered from the same consignment reads: “Incubate loose cells at +30 °C; twenty-one days to emergence; board out at first purple; hold to fifteen per cent before broadcast.” The recovered stock-label parses not as a species but as a function: the leaf-mason of the fodder-seed. Because the whole managed range drew on one repeatedly shipped source, it carried, in Ibarra’s sense, a single filament — the seed crop of a continent turning on one bottlenecked bloodline of bee. The consignment that preserves it survived because the truck that carried it was abandoned in a wet ditch rather than a dry one; the tannin-rich leaf linings and the anoxic water arrested decay, and the dormant larvae held enough tissue to sequence.

The misread velocity

The first reconstructions fitted the northward advance of the cell assemblages to a diffusion wave and found it plausible: the front moved at very roughly the pace at which Late Combustion species were independently observed to track warming poleward — of order sixteen kilometres a decade — and the fit invited the reading that here was a pollinator following its climate envelope. Adeyemi’s re-analysis dissolved it. A population spreading under its own diffusion leaves a genetic cline — near stocks more alike than far ones, structure accumulating with distance. The assemblage has none: cells five hundred kilometres and forty years apart are one panmictic stock, with no isolation by distance at all. The advance was not a wave but a series of jumps from a single source — stratified, human-mediated dispersal — and the organism could not have held any of the ground it appears on without being incubated, boarded, and released onto it each spring. Its distribution is therefore not a climate proxy. It is a provisioning record: the bee is where the seed-farms were, and the seed-farms were retreating — poleward, inland, upland — through the +120s pT (the 2060s CE), on the same gradient as the hands that tended them. What the assemblage cannot say is what became of the line once the last consignment went un-incubated: whether it failed for want of provisioning, or held on feral in some ground the record no longer reaches. The northern cells mark where the shipping stopped. Whether they also mark where the bee stopped is not in them.

Provenance and ground

The type material is the diverted +112 pT consignment: a single lot of loose overwintering cells, waterlogged in anoxic ditch sediment, from which both the leaf-lined cell structures and a whole-organism genome were recovered. Halvorsen’s taphonomy attributes the survival to the leaf mastic’s tannins and the exclusion of oxygen; comparable dry-shelved lots did not keep. The wider distribution is read from disarticulated cells in dated sediments, not from a body; the assemblage is abundant but geographically narrow, and over-samples the tilled ground it was shipped to.
