# The Fifteen-Hundred Shell

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a flight-software constants file in the Code Deposit, an intercepted ground-loop voice log in the Wasatch Corpus, a survey of buried gateway-site remains, and a modern tracking catalogue, and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Fifteen-Hundred Shell is the Historical Faculty's name for the 648 retired objects of a Late Combustion Age communications constellation, raised in +112 to +114 pT (2057–2059 CE) from 1,200 km to 1,500 km instead of being brought down, and read for some centuries as a votive ring.** The orbital geometry is record, recomputed and confirmed by tracking; the votive reading was a reconstruction, and the cost that the raising deferred is stated in the operator's own filing.

---

**The Fifteen-Hundred Shell is not a monument, not an offering, and not a calendar.** It is the residue of a disposal decision: a constellation of 648 relay satellites, in 18 orbital planes of 36, retired at the end of its second generation by a burn that cost less propellant than the alternative and left every object aloft. The discipline's earlier name for it, the Ring of the Laid-Down, assigned it a ritual purpose from the regularity of its spacing and from the liturgical sound of the recovered exchange that attends its retirement. Both inferences were corrected in stages between 2790 and 2905 CE, and the first is still printed in almanacs. Of the 648 objects, the most recent catalogue tracks 417. The remaining 231 are neither confirmed down nor confirmed up.

## Geometry

The constellation entered service from +80 pT (2025 CE) at 1,200 km altitude and 87.9° inclination. At that height the orbital radius is 7,578 km and the period 109.4 minutes; the circular speed is 7.25 km/s. Thirty-six satellites in each plane sat at 10° of phase from their neighbours, 1,323 km apart along the track, and the eighteen planes were spaced 10° apart in right ascension. The numbers are set by the footprint of one satellite's beams on the ground and by the requirement that a polar gateway always have one overhead; they carry no symbolic weight.

They do carry a physical one. Nodal precession, the slow westward turning of an orbital plane caused by Earth's equatorial bulge, goes as the cosine of inclination, and at 87.9° it is small: about 0.2° per day at 1,200 km. More to the point, every plane shares the same altitude and inclination and therefore the same rate. The pattern turns as a rigid body. A scatter of objects at differing altitudes would have lost its order within decades. This one kept it for nine centuries, which is the property the votive reading rested on.

## The retirement burn

The flight-software constants file in the Code Deposit holds a disposal routine with two modes, `lower` and `raise`, and a field that selects between them. The first mode needed a single retrograde burn of about 305 m/s at the 1,200 km point to drop the perigee to 50 km and bring the object into the atmosphere within one or two orbits. The second needed two burns of about 69 m/s each, a total of about 138 m/s, to place the object on a circular orbit at 1,500 km, where the period is 116 minutes. For a chemical thruster with an exhaust speed near 2.0 km/s, the rocket equation prices the first at 14.1 per cent of the satellite's mass in propellant and the second at 6.7 per cent. The difference, 7.4 points of launch mass multiplied over 648 spacecraft, was the whole saving. The field is set to `raise` in the surviving file, with a commit comment reading only "per fleet review".

At 1,500 km atmospheric drag is too weak to bring an object down on any timescale a filing would be written for; the residual lifetime depends on area-to-mass ratio and on the eleven-year modulation of the solar cycle, and runs to centuries or longer. The operator's retirement filing, partly recovered from the same consignment, gives the figure as "in excess of one thousand years". The cost of the disposal was thereby moved from the operator's propellant budget to the orbital shell, and from the decade of retirement to the following millennium. The filing records it under the heading *compliant*. The shell lies near the lower edge of the inner radiation belt's proton population, where solar cells and electronics age faster than at lower altitudes, and the objects have since fragmented unevenly.

## The exchange

The ground-loop log, intercepted as a minor stream in the Wasatch Corpus, preserves the sequence for one satellite, in a flat repeated format:

> CTRL: Plane eleven, slot four. Retirement burn one. Go, no-go.
> PROP: Go.
> ADCS: Go.
> POWER: Go.
> CTRL: Burn one, go. Mark.
> [burn, 41 s]
> CTRL: Burn two, go. Mark.
> [burn, 44 s]
> CTRL: Apogee fifteen hundred. Passivate.
> PROP: Vent complete. Safed.
> POWER: Batteries safed.
> CTRL: Plane eleven, slot four, retired. Next.

The same text recurs, with the slot number changed, for 648 sequences across forty-one days, in a call-and-response of three fixed answers. The earliest translators rendered *Go* as a dismissal, *Safed* as a cognate of *sacred*, *Mark* as a placing, and *retired* as laid to rest. Passivation, the venting of residual propellant and the discharge of batteries so that a dead spacecraft cannot burst, was read as a blessing. The roster header of the last session lists four staff.

## The gateway burials

The reading drew support from a second source. The ground gateway for the polar planes stood at 78° N, and the remains of its staff settlement show runs of homozygosity, long stretches of identical ancestry inherited from both parents, consistent with a small, closed breeding population over some six generations. The Faculty's early reading took this for a hereditary priesthood. The relaxation of that structure in the later burials, from about +118 pT (2063 CE), follows the arrival of a handful of unrelated lineages in the order their travel routes reached the settlement, not any change of observance. The data do not say whether the relaxation is a loosening or a flight.

## Correction and what remains

Marchetti showed in 2790 that the three-part exchange is the period's ordinary readiness poll. Okonkwo identified the polling format from the timestamps in the log itself. Halvorsen computed the lifetimes of the shell from the objects' recovered mass and drag area, and found that the lower planes should have begun to decay about 2400 CE, in a spread of a few centuries thereafter. Ranganathan observed that the log is mostly the work of machines reading to machines, and that a human closing line is absent from it.

The tracking catalogue does not list plane eleven, slot four. Its predicted position on the next pass of the gateway is published, and no instrument has yet been pointed at it.

## Provenance and ground

The constants file, the filing and the voice log were recovered separately and joined by identifier; no surviving record states that they describe one fleet. The gateway burials were excavated in 2630 CE. The orbital quantities are recomputed, not recovered.
