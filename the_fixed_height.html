The Fixed Height

Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a seized two-ball centrifugal governor raised from the flooded sump of a spent tin working and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.

The Fixed Height is the Historical Faculty’s name for the reading of a seized centrifugal governor, raised from the sump of a spent tin working, as a record of the last speed of the engine it governed. The governor and the angle of its arms are record; the engine, the seizure, and the speed are reconstruction.

At the 40-fathom level of a flooded tin working at 50°13′ N, 5°16′ W, the arms of a two-ball centrifugal governor stand at 34° from their spindle, held there by a sleeve that seized on it while turning. A governor of this form is a conical pendulum: its balls ride at a height above their own plane of h = g/ω², fixed by the speed of the shaft and by nothing else — not the mass of the balls, not the load on the engine. Arms of 305 mm at 34° give a height of 253 mm and a shaft speed of 59.5 turns a minute, the only speed of an Early Combustion engine the Faculty holds from metal rather than paper.

The stopped clock

A stopped engine lets its arms fall to the lower stop; arms found above it were held. Halvorsen, E. finds the sleeve scored where it seized, metal on moving metal, under a siderite skin that formed only after the water rose. On the stopped-clock reading — the period’s own method, which fixed its earthquakes and detonations to the minute — the angle is the shaft speed at the moment of seizure. The standing objection is that a governor whose sleeve has seized has ceased to be a governor, and the speed it records is the speed at which it stopped knowing the engine’s.

The height

The device was the object on which the period first wrote the mathematics of feedback stability, in −77 pT (1868 CE); the analysis linearised the governor about its running height and dropped the dry friction of the sleeve — the friction that, as machining improved, fell until the better-made governors of the following decades hunted where the cruder ones had held. The theory written on the governor could not hold on the governor. The friction that stopped this one fixed its height, and the height is insensitive in exactly the way the period complained of: the whole sweep from stop to stop spans nine turns a minute.

The working’s pumps were drawn in the autumn of −62 pT (1883 CE).

Provenance and ground

The governor is the single witness, raised from standing anoxic water below the 40-fathom level that had held its iron in siderite and magnetite; the bracket is cast with the year −104 pT (1841 CE). The height relation, the analysis of −77 pT, and the hunting of the improved governors are the period’s own; the arm length and angle are measured; the speed follows from them.
