# The One Plant Stele

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a two-faced granite stele at an upland burial ground and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **A two-faced granite stele records eleven hundred named varieties of a sterile fruit-plant and, on its reverse, a transfer manifest for a crewed vehicle.** The inscriptions are record; the reading that joins the two faces is reconstruction.

---

**Eleven hundred named varieties, one plant** — the stele's obverse is a catalogue of cultivars of a sweet, seedless, triple-chromosome fruit-plant, grown on every wet lowland and propagated only by cutting suckers from the parent mat. A clone has no sexual generations; every cutting is the same individual. The lineage is dated from first planting at +61 pT (2006 CE) to the first soil-borne wilt at +118 pT (2063 CE) and the last entry at +150 pT (2095 CE). Its name survives nowhere in the record but the stone, where it is cut as *ARDMOOR MILD*. The reverse face lists fourteen sealed tissue-culture vessels loaded for a Mars transfer.

## The obverse

The catalogue gives 1,100 trade names, each with a growing district. A footing line gives a total of 380 million mats under cultivation. The fungus that caused the wilt persists in soil as resting spores for decades, rides on boots, tools and irrigation water, and has no resistant host to be stopped by in a field where every plant is one individual. The stone records the outbreak doubling each season for nine seasons. At that rate one infected mat becomes five hundred and twelve.

The catalogue also quotes a certification assay, devised by the breeders' guild to prove that one named variety differed from another, and paid for from the lineage's own rents. Applied to the lineage, it returned *identical* for all 1,100 names. The names were bud sports of one clone, and the method built to tell them apart could not tell them apart. The stone lists the assay's fee schedule on the next line.

## The reverse

The manifest lists fourteen vessels. From a 300 km circular orbit, which moves at 7.73 km/s, a Hohmann transfer to Mars requires about 3.6 km/s of departure velocity change and 258.8 days in flight; departure windows recur every 779.9 days. With propellant exhausting at 4.4 km/s, each additional km/s multiplies the departing mass by 1.25, so that 3.6 km/s costs a mass ratio of 2.3. Fourteen vessels were one genotype because a second genotype was a second kilogram, and every kilogram was paid for in that ratio.

The two faces are ruled on one grid. Both columns are geometric: the wilt multiplies by a constant factor per season, and the vehicle's mass by a constant factor per km/s. Marchetti reads the stone as one commission. The corpus-critical school reads two monuments, one dressed down and reused.

## Recovered exchange

Below the manifest, in a third hand or none, the stone carries a question-and-answer cut in capitals:

> HOW MANY ARE THE VARIETIES. — ELEVEN HUNDRED.
> HOW MANY ARE THE PLANTS. — ONE.
> HOW MANY ARE THE MOUTHS. — [illegible; surface spalled]

The cutting cannot be classed as mason's or generator's. The count of mats is the only quantity the stone supplies that bears on population, and it cannot say whether a failed planting fed fewer people, or the same people elsewhere.

---

## Provenance and ground

The stele stands in an upland burial ground; the grave beneath it was not opened. The face with the exchange has lost its last line to spalling.
