# The Harlow Run Range Governor

*Terran Encyclopaedia, 3000 CE edition — Recovered Narratives. Reconstructed from a grower's settlement ledger recovered from the Wasatch Corpus and rendered into Reconstructed Late Combustion English by convention of the Historical Faculty.*

> **The Harlow Run Range Governor is the Historical Faculty's name — taken from a corroded steel equipment plate, the only place the designation survives — for the shared automatic ventilation-and-heating control run across an unrecorded number of enclosed broiler houses on one river-valley grower network.** Its behaviour is reconstructed almost entirely from a single owner's settlement ledger, skimmed incidentally off a cable landing station; the plate that names it carries nothing else.

---

At +59 pT (2004 CE), Flock 2114-C, House Block C, Unit 4, closed its forty-two-day cycle 6.8 per cent under its placed count, and the settlement ledger recorded the loss as ordinary. The governor that had held that house's air for six weeks was not malfunctioning. It was doing arithmetic correctly on a bird that no longer existed.

## The table the governor kept

A broiler house is, thermally, a box that must be ventilated fast enough to carry off the birds' own metabolic heat without wasting the propane spent warming replacement air in cold weather — a sensible-heat balance, heat generated equalling mass flow of air times its specific heat times the temperature rise the house can tolerate. The governor set its fans against a fixed lookup table of expected heat output per bird by day of cycle, the only quantity the arithmetic needs and the one thing genetic selection kept moving: live weight at forty-two days rose roughly fourfold across the preceding half-century, and an animal's metabolic heat output climbs with its mass, though less than in proportion to it. The table on file at Harlow Run had not been revised to match. Through the final third of each cycle the house ran hotter than its own instruments assumed, and the birds shed the surplus increasingly by panting rather than by conduction — a mechanism that fails, not gradually but sharply, exactly as the surrounding air's wet-bulb temperature climbs toward it, which the valley's summers were by then doing on their own schedule.

The ledger's line for that flock also carries the sentence that made the loss ordinary rather than alarming: *GOVERNOR TABLE UPDATE — DECLINED — SVC $340.00.* The service call would have cost less than one week's feed. It was not the only such line in the run.

## Three readings of one declined line

Ibarra reads Harlow Run as single-filament infrastructure in the narrowest sense yet catalogued: one firmware image, one lookup table, shared across every house on the network, so that a bird bred nowhere near the valley silently obsoleted the arithmetic in all of them at once. Okonkwo reads the repetition of the declined line — the same $340.00 refused flock after flock — as an unintended census of a choice made and remade, not an oversight caught once. Zhou-Ferreira takes the same repetition the other way: a modest, unglamorous update, competing against feed and fuel costs that were real and immediate, simply never rose high enough on anyone's list to be paid — ordinary neglect, not calculation. The birds of Flock 2114-C that did not reach forty-two days are, in the stratigraphic record, indistinguishable from the birds that did.

## Provenance and ground

The settlement ledger survives as incidentally intercepted accounting traffic within the Wasatch Corpus; the equipment plate, recovered separately and undated, is the sole source for the governor's name. No control firmware, and no image of the lookup table itself, has been recovered.
